# A Complete Guide to Total War: Three Kingdoms

## The Campaign Map

The campaign map is your empire. Everything — armies, cities, trade, characters — lives on this single screen. Understanding how it works is the foundation of every campaign.

### Commanderies, Settlements & Resources

China is divided into **commanderies** — the equivalent of provinces. Each commandery contains a **capital** (the main city) and one or more **resource settlements** (smaller towns producing copper, iron, horses, fish, or timber).

Resource settlements are why you go to war. Copper reduces construction costs across your entire faction. Iron unlocks better infantry units. Horses unlock better cavalry. A commandery with a horse pasture is worth fighting for; a barren plateau is not. Look at the resource icon next to each settlement before you decide where to expand — it tells you exactly what you're gaining.

Each commandery also has a **population** value. As population grows, you can upgrade buildings to higher tiers. Population grows fastest with high food surplus and high public order. Starvation and unrest stop growth completely.

**Build order that works for every commandery:**
1. **Farm first** — always. Food surplus is non-negotiable. Without it you get starvation, unrest, and revolts.
2. **Income building second** — market for commerce, workshop for industry. Match it to the commandery's resources (e.g., a port commandery wants a trade port, not a workshop).
3. **Public order third** — temple, Confucian school, or garrison. Corruption and expansion generate unrest. You need a counter before the unrest arrives, not after.

### Characters

Characters are the most important resource in Three Kingdoms. A level-6 Vanguard general with good traits is worth more than an entire army. Lose your loyal generals because of mismanagement and your campaign will fall apart whether you notice it or not.

Every character has a **class** (Commander, Vanguard, Champion, Strategist, Sentinel), a **satisfaction** score, and a set of **traits** earned through gameplay. Satisfaction is loyalty — drop below 0 and the character conspires against you or defects to another faction. Several things affect satisfaction:

- **Court position** — the single most powerful satisfaction tool. Every character wants a title. Give one to anyone with low satisfaction.
- **Salary** — increases automatically with rank. You can raise it manually but it costs gold.
- **Ancillaries** — weapons, armour, and accessories. Each gives +2–5 satisfaction on top of their stat bonuses. Give your spare items to borderline-loyal characters.
- **Events and dilemmas** — choices you make in story events can swing satisfaction by +/-20 for specific characters. Think before you pick a side.
- **Idleness** — characters gain a "Complacent" trait if left idle for many turns, which gives a permanent satisfaction penalty. Never leave a good general doing nothing.

You manage characters through several systems:

- **Assignments** let you dispatch characters to settlements for temporary bonuses (food, commerce, satisfaction, etc.). This is how you keep idle characters useful without paying them a court position.
- **Administrators** are assigned to individual commanderies. They reduce corruption and provide bonuses based on their traits. A Sentinel administrator in a wealthy province can double its income.
- **Court positions** are faction-level titles (Heir, Prime Minister, Chancellor, etc.) that grant powerful faction-wide bonuses. The Prime Minister's "+satisfaction to all" trait can single-handedly stabilise a large empire.
- **The family tree** tracks your faction leader, their spouse, children, and adopted members. Family members are permanently loyal. Use adoption (available through character events) to permanently secure talented but disloyal outsiders.

### Government & Reforms

The **reforms tree** is Three Kingdoms' technology system. Unlike a traditional tech tree, reforms unlock in turns — each reform takes 5 turns to research. This means you can't rush a military advantage; you need to plan your research path early and commit.

Each reform gives a permanent faction-wide bonus. The tree is divided into branches (military, economy, administration, commerce) and you typically want to spread points across branches rather than going deep in one. However:

- **Governance reforms** (purple branch) reduce corruption and boost satisfaction. These scale with empire size — the bigger you get, the more valuable they become.
- **Commerce reforms** (green branch) boost income and trade. These compound over time — invest early and the returns accumulate across the entire campaign.
- **Military reforms** unlock better units and general abilities. Important, but don't neglect economy for them. A tier-3 economy can fund tier-2 armies; a tier-3 army with tier-1 economy runs out of gold by turn 30.

**Government type** determines which court positions are available and how your satisfaction system works. Changing government type is expensive (causes a -15 satisfaction penalty to everyone for several turns) so choose one that fits your playstyle and stick with it.

## Diplomacy

Diplomacy in Three Kingdoms is not a side system. It is your primary defence and your most efficient weapon. A well-negotiated peace saves you an army. A marriage alliance can flip a neutral neighbour into a loyal ally. A food-for-gold trade deal funds an entire building queue.

Every diplomatic action depends on **relations** — a score between -100 and +100 that represents how much that faction likes you. Relations build through trade, shared enemies, gifts, and marriage. They decay through war, broken promises, and proximity (neighbouring factions naturally distrust each other).

Here is what each diplomatic tool actually does and when to use it:

- **Trade agreement** — requires a connected route (roads, ports, or shared borders leading to each other's capitals). Generates gold per turn. Sign with everyone who'll accept. The gold adds up fast and, critically, trade improves relations over time.
- **Non-aggression pact** — the most important defensive tool. An NAP prevents a faction from declaring war on you. Sign them with neighbours you don't plan to fight for the next 30 turns. Breaking an NAP damages your reliability.
- **Military access** — lets allies move through your territory. Don't give it out unless you genuinely need their army on your land. Access is often used as a prelude to invasion by untrustworthy AI.
- **Coalition** — a defensive alliance of multiple factions. If anyone attacks a coalition member, all members join the war. This is the best deterrent in the game. Form coalitions early with your neighbours.
- **Military alliance** — an offensive and defensive pact. You join all of their wars (and they join yours). Military alliances are dangerous — the AI will drag you into wars you don't want. Use sparingly.
- **Marriage** — binds two families. Between your faction and another, it's an alliance-level bond. Within your faction, marrying a character into your family makes them permanently loyal.
- **Vassalage** — subjugates another faction. They pay you tribute, can't declare war independently, and must join your wars. Use this instead of conquering — a vassal is a buffer state that generates gold without adding to your corruption or public order burden.

**Food-for-money deals** deserve special mention. Many AI factions run food deficits. You can trade excess food for lump sums of gold or per-turn income. This is the easiest way to generate early-game cash. Offer 10-20 food per turn for 500-1000 gold to any faction with low reserves.

**Reliability** is your most fragile diplomatic stat. It drops every time you break a treaty, attack an ally, or declare war within a truce cooldown. Low reliability makes every other faction refuse to deal with you — they will extort you, refuse trade, and declare opportunistic wars. Never break a treaty unless absolutely necessary. Never declare war on an ally unless you've already wrecked that campaign and need to salvage it.

## Battles

### Unit Matchups (The Combat Triangle)

Every unit in Three Kingdoms fits into a rock-paper-scissors system. Winning battles is about bringing the right counter to each enemy unit.

| Your Unit | Beats | Loses To | Strategy |
|-----------|-------|----------|----------|
| **Spear Infantry** | Cavalry | Shock Infantry, Missiles | Frontline anchor. Hold position, brace against charges. |
| **Sword Infantry** | Shock Infantry | Missiles, Cavalry | Flexible line. Decent in melee, weak to cavalry flanking. |
| **Shock Infantry** | Spear Infantry | Sword Infantry, Missiles | Two-handed weapons, armour-piercing. Kill armoured targets. |
| **Cavalry (Melee)** | Missiles, Ranged | Spear Infantry | Flanking and pursuit. Never charge spears head-on. |
| **Cavalry (Shock)** | Missiles, Ranged | Spear Infantry | Cycle charge: charge, withdraw, charge again. Massive impact. |
| **Missile Infantry** | Spear/Sword Infantry | Cavalry | Protect with frontline. Fire into enemy flanks for morale damage. |
| **Artillery** | Dense formations, Towers | Cavalry | Siege-breaking. Use on enemy blobs and gate towers. |

**The golden rule:** Spear holds the line, cavalry flanks, missiles kill from safety, shock infantry breaks through.

### Formations & When to Use Them

| Formation | Use When | Effect |
|-----------|----------|--------|
| **Shield Wall** | Expecting frontal missile fire | +100% shield block chance, -speed |
| **Spear Wall** | Expecting cavalry charge | Massive charge negation, anti-large bonus |
| **Diamond** | Marching through danger | +50% speed, -defence — move through a kill zone |
| **Wedge** | Cavalry breakthrough | Charge bonus increased, armour reduced |
| **Testudo** | Approaching archer towers | Blocks overhead missiles |
| **Loose** | Under artillery fire | Reduces AoE damage, -melee defence |
| **Circle** | Surrounded | 360-degree defence, no movement |

### Terrain & Positioning

| Terrain | Effect | How to Use |
|---------|--------|------------|
| **High Ground** | +10% missile damage, +5 melee damage, morale bonus | Always take the hill before engaging. |
| **Forest** | Blocks line of sight, -20% missile accuracy | Hide ambush units. Negates enemy archers. |
| **River** | Slows movement, exhausts units | Defend the far bank. Attack while enemy is crossing. |
| **Choke Point** | Numerical advantage negated | Small elite force can hold against a much larger army. |
| **Settlement Walls** | Garrison missile bonus, gate defence | Use towers to weaken attackers before sallying out. |
| **Bridge** | Forces 1v1 engagements | Best defensive position in the game. |

**How to win a land battle:**
1. **Pre-battle:** Deploy on high ground. Put spears in front, missiles behind, cavalry on flanks.
2. **Engagement:** Let enemy come to you (defensive advantage). Spear wall vs cavalry. Shield wall vs missiles.
3. **Flank:** Once lines are engaged, send cavalry around the side. Charge into the enemy's rear — this causes massive morale damage and routes units instantly.
4. **General snipe:** Focus missiles on the enemy general. If the general dies, nearby units take huge morale penalties.
5. **Cleanup:** Once enemy morale breaks, pursue with cavalry. A routing unit that escapes can fight again — don't let them.

### Morale Mechanics

Morale is the most important stat in Total War. A high-stat unit with broken morale is useless.

| Factor | Effect on Morale |
|--------|------------------|
| **General nearby** | +morale aura (radius increases with rank) |
| **General killed** | Massive morale penalty to nearby units |
| **Flanked (side hit)** | Moderate morale damage |
| **Rear charge** | Severe morale damage (1 charge can route a unit) |
| **Routing allies nearby** | Cascading morale loss |
| **Exhaustion** | Significant morale penalty |
| **On fire** | Morale penalty + attrition |
| **Heroic victory** | Permanent morale buff for that army |

**To make enemies route:** Kill/injure their general, flank with cavalry, then focus missiles on the wavering unit. Wavering → routing → chain route.

### General Classes on the Battlefield

| Class | Role | Key Ability |
|-------|------|-------------|
| **Commander** | Army morale anchor | "+satisfaction" aura, army replenishment |
| **Vanguard** | Cavalry commander | Charge bonus, cavalry buffs |
| **Champion** | Duelist, infantry killer | Personal duelling skills, high melee damage |
| **Strategist** | Ranged support, formations | Fire arrows, smoke screen, night battles |
| **Sentinel** | Defensive line, garrison buff | Melee defence, garrison bonuses |

**In Romance mode** generals are superheroes — they can single-handedly kill hundreds of troops. Use them aggressively but don't let them get surrounded.
**In Records mode** generals are bodyguard units (heavy cavalry). Powerful but mortal — use as elite cavalry, not invincible heroes.

### Army Composition Templates

**Balanced (recommended for learning):**
- 2 Spear Infantry (frontline anchor)
- 2 Sword/Shock Infantry (melee line)
- 2 Ji Militia (anti-cavalry reserve)
- 2 Archer Militia (ranged damage)
- 2 Jian Sword Cavalry or Sabre Cavalry (flanking)
- General retinues fill the rest

**Cavalry Focus (aggressive):**
- 2 Spear Infantry (minimal frontline)
- 4 Cavalry units (mix of shock and melee)
- 2 Archers (pin the enemy)
- Everything in general retinues as cavalry

**Defensive (counter-attack):**
- 4 Spear Infantry (Shield Wall)
- 4 Archers/Crossbows (behind the wall)
- 2 Cavalry (chase routing units)
- 1 Strategist general (fire arrows, formations)

### Siege Tactics

**Attacking:**
1. **Scout** — check which walls have towers. Destroy towers with artillery before advancing.
2. **Breach** — use trebuchets to open wall sections. Send shock infantry through the breach.
3. **Multiple fronts** — attack two walls simultaneously. The AI splits its garrison.
4. **Ladders** — cheap but costly (your climbing units take heavy losses). Use ladders only on weakly-defended walls.
5. **Gate** — battering ram opens the gate. Your cavalry floods in. Rush the capture point.
6. **Victory** — secure the centre square. The garrison surrenders once you hold it.

**Defending:**
1. **Towers first** — the AI will target your towers. Repair them between assaults.
2. **Burning oil** — gates have oil that incinerates attackers. Save it for elite units.
3. **Sally out** — if the enemy has no siege engines, attack them outside the walls with your garrison + relief army.
4. **Attrition** — if outnumbered, let the enemy starve. Disease and desertion weaken them over time.

## Espionage

Espionage is Three Kingdoms' invisible weapon. A single well-placed spy can dismantle an empire more efficiently than an army. The system works by sending your characters undercover into enemy factions.

To embed a spy: select any character you can assign, choose "Spy," and pick a target faction. The character disappears from your roster and appears in the target faction's court. From that point, they operate independently — building cover, gathering intelligence, and eventually performing actions against the host.

**Building cover:** Every spy has a "cover" rating. Higher cover means the enemy doesn't suspect them. Gain cover by performing mundane actions — revealing the faction's army positions, reporting on their income, or simply waiting. Each action has a risk percentage; the riskier the action, the more cover it burns.

**Actions available:**
- **Reveal army positions** (low risk) — simple and safe. Good for building cover.
- **Sabotage buildings** (medium risk) — damages an enemy settlement's buildings. Slows their economy.
- **Steal technologies** (medium risk) — you gain a reform for free. Huge value.
- **Assassinate a character** (high risk) — kills a targeted character. Use against their faction leader or a dangerous general about to attack you.
- **Incite rebellion** (extreme risk) — spawns a rebel army in the target province. Can cripple an enemy's backline.

**Your own counter-intelligence:** The AI uses spies against you too. Assign a high-cunning character as an administrator in each commandery — they will reveal enemy spies operating in that region. If you suddenly have public order problems in a stable province or can't see where an enemy army is moving, you likely have an enemy spy.

A spy who becomes the enemy faction leader (through assassination and political maneuvering) can choose to reveal themselves and immediately absorb the entire faction into yours. This takes 50+ turns of careful work but it's the most efficient way to win in the entire game.

## Economy

Your economy is a triangle: **food**, **income**, and **public order**. You cannot neglect any of the three without the triangle collapsing. Every building and reform decision should be evaluated against all three.

**Food** is the foundation. Each army consumes food. Each settlement with a population above 0 consumes food. Running a food deficit causes starvation, which tanks public order and prevents population growth. The minimum safe food surplus is +5. As you expand, aim for +10–20 to buffer against sudden consumption spikes (like recruiting a new general).

**Income** comes from three sources:
- **Commerce** — trade agreements and market buildings. Scales with trade routes. Best for coastal and border factions.
- **Industry** — workshops and ports. Steady, reliable, doesn't need external trade partners. Best for isolated factions.
- **Taxation** — your ongoing income from owned settlements, adjusted by your tax rate. Higher taxes = more income but slower population growth and lower public order. Keep the tax rate at normal unless you're in crisis.

**Corruption** is the silent killer of large empires. Every commandery beyond your first adds a corruption percentage that eats into income. An empire of 10 commanderies may lose 30–50% of all income to corruption. Reduce it with:
- Assigning administrators (each admin reduces corruption in their commandery)
- Reforms in the Governance tree (purple branch)
- Buildings that reduce corruption (state workshops, granaries)
- Characters with the "Honest" or "Incorruptible" trait

**Trade** multiplies your income but requires infrastructure. To trade with a faction you need a connected road or sea route from your capital to theirs. Build roads in every commandery. Upgrade your capital's trade port if coastal. Each trade agreement you sign increases the value of your market buildings.

## Victory Conditions

Three Kingdoms has a layered victory system. You don't need to paint the entire map to win.

**Short campaign:** Control X territories (depends on faction) and achieve a prestige milestone. Typically achievable by turn 80–100. A good stopping point for your first campaign after learning the fundamentals.

**Long campaign:** Control three key commanderies (the capitals of the three historical kingdoms). Takes 120–180 turns. This is the "real" campaign — you'll experience the full arc from minor warlord to emperor.

**The Three Kingdoms phase** triggers when any three factions reach King or Emperor prestige level. At this point, the game's diplomatic landscape freezes — the three kingdoms become permanent rivals. Victory comes from conquering or vassalising both rival capitals.

**Prestige** is the hidden meter driving victory progression. Every settlement captured, battle won, and reform researched adds prestige. Reaching certain prestige thresholds unlocks kingdom/emperor status and triggers new campaign phases. Focus on steady prestige gain rather than rushing — declaring emperor too early before your armies are ready invites a coalition against you.

## Turn Checklist (Every Turn)

Before ending each turn, run through these six checks. They take 15 seconds and prevent 90% of campaign-altering mistakes.

1. **Food —** Is your surplus above +5? If not, cancel a building queue and start a farm. Do not end the turn with a deficit.
2. **Characters —** Is anyone below 60 satisfaction? Give them an ancillary, a court position, or an assignment. Scroll the full character list every turn — don't just check the top screen.
3. **Armies —** Are all your armies doing something? An idle army is an army costing you upkeep for nothing. Replenish in a settlement, move toward a target, or recruit.
4. **Diplomacy —** Are your trade slots full? Check every faction you've met — if "Trade: Accept" is green, send the deal. Missing trade agreements is missing free gold.
5. **Buildings —** Is there an empty building slot? Start something. An empty slot earns zero gold and produces zero food. Even a tier-1 farm is better than an empty lot.
6. **Spies —** If you have no spy in play, send one. Embed them in your most threatening neighbour's court. Building cover takes time — start now.

## Zero to Master Training Path

### Phase 0: Setup & Mindset

**Goal:** Start correctly and avoid common beginner traps.

- **Records vs Romance:** Records mode for more realistic battles; Romance for cinematic hero duels. Both are valid — try both.
- **Recommended first faction:** Liu Bei (strong generals + diplomacy) or Sun Jian (safer southern start).
- **Difficulty:** Start on Normal or Hard. Skip Legendary until you've completed all waves.
- **Tutorials:** Turn on in-game tooltips and watch the official Total War Academy videos (linked in main menu).

**Key mindset:** This is a game of characters + diplomacy + economy, not just battles.

### Phase 1: Core Fundamentals

**Goal:** Understand the basic loop and survive the early game.

#### Must-Learn Mechanics

- **Commanderies & Settlements** — Every commandery has a capital plus resource settlements. Food and money are the foundation of everything.
- **Characters** — Five classes: Commander, Vanguard, Champion, Strategist, Sentinel. Each has unique roles on the battlefield and in administration.
- **Assignments** — Put characters as Administrators, in Court positions, or as Spies. Never leave good characters idle.
- **Armies** — 3 generals max per army (with retinues). Learn the difference between Militia and Professional units.
- **Basic Diplomacy** — Trade agreements, non-aggression pacts, and coalitions. Food-for-money deals are your best friend early.

#### Training Exercises

1. Play one full campaign as Liu Bei or Sun Jian focusing only on economy and not over-expanding.
2. Manually fight every battle for the first 30-40 turns so you learn unit strengths.
3. Never auto-resolve important battles until you understand why you win or lose.

**Success criteria:** Reach turn 50-60 without going bankrupt or being destroyed. Understand why food surplus matters.
