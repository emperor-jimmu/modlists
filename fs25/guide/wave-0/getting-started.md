# Getting Started — A Complete Beginner's Guide to FS25

## 1. First Launch

### Graphics Settings

- Resolution: Native monitor resolution
- FSR/DLSS: On if available (boosts FPS)
- Grass Draw Distance: Medium (high impact on performance)
- Shadows: High (big visual impact, moderate performance cost)
- Volumetric Fog: Off (high performance cost, minimal gameplay impact)

## 2. Your First 15 Minutes

When you first load into the game, don't buy anything. Don't start a field. Just breathe.

### Walk the Farm

- Open the map (M) and study your land — note field sizes, road access, sell points
- Walk (not drive) to each building — learn what's where, check the silo capacity, note which animal pens exist
- Find the nearest sell point and drive there once (memorize the route for later)
- Check the price menu (P) — note which crops are selling high today, then check the calendar for what can still be planted

### Your First Decision: What to Grow

- **New players**: Start with wheat or barley. Simplest equipment, reliable profits, sellable year-round. You cannot go wrong here.
- **If you're feeling bold**: Canola requires the same equipment as grains but pays more. It's the same workflow with a better margin.
- **Avoid early**: Potatoes, sugar beets, corn silage. These need expensive specialized gear. They're a trap in Week 1.

### Map Your Route

Before you touch a machine, walk the route from your silo to the sell point. Note bridge clearances, sharp turns, and hills — you'll be driving this route loaded, and getting stuck costs you real time and money. Width matters: a loaded trailer behind a tractor takes up more road than you think. Turn off traffic initially if you want to learn without cars honking at you, but turn it back on once you've got the basics — it adds tension that makes the game feel alive.

### First Purchase Checklist

1. **Tractor** (medium, ~150–200 HP — enough for basic implements plus room to grow)
2. **Cultivator or disc harrow** (matches your tractor's horsepower)
3. **Seeder** (direct seeder if budget allows — cultivates AND seeds in one pass)
4. **Fertilizer spreader** (cheapest option — solid fertilizer, one stage at a time)
5. **Weeder** (cheapest mechanical weeder — only works on early-stage weeds)
6. **Trailer** (for transport — match capacity to your field size)
7. **Combine harvester + grain header** (this is your biggest purchase — consider leasing your first harvest to test which combine size feels right)

Don't buy everything at once. Buy as you need it. A tractor + cultivator + seeder gets you through the first week. Harvest equipment waits until the crop is ripe.

### Take a Loan... or Don't

The Advanced Bank System mod (included in Wave 0) gives you better loan management than vanilla. But here's the key principle: **only borrow for assets that generate income**. A tractor that lets you farm faster pays for itself. A decorative shed does not. Keep your loan-to-asset ratio below 50% — if your equipment is worth $200,000, don't owe more than $100,000. The interest will eat you alive otherwise.

## 3. The Farm Yard

Your farm starts with:

- A **farmhouse** (no function, just aesthetic)
- A **workshop** (repair vehicles — costs money)
- A **silo** (stores grain, grass, and chaff)
- A **manure pit** (stores manure from animals)
- Empty **animal pens** (buy animals later)

## 4. Basic Field Work

The core farming loop:

### Step 1: Plow / Cultivate

- **Plowing**: Required every 3 harvests (or if field has rocks/weeds). Creates new field edges.
- **Cultivating**: Prepares seedbed. Faster than plowing. Required between harvests.
- Which one? After harvest, cultivate (faster). After 3 harvests or when the field says "needs plowing," plow.

### Step 2: Seeding / Planting

- Use a seeder or planter
- Different crops require different seasons:
    - **Spring**: Wheat, barley, oats, canola, potatoes, sugar beets
    - **Summer**: Corn, sunflowers, soybeans
    - **Autumn**: Wheat (for next year), grass (anytime)
- The in-game calendar shows ideal planting windows

### Step 3: Fertilizing

- Two stages: fertilize after seeding, then again after crop sprouts
- Each stage gives +50% yield (at standard difficulty)
- Methods: fertilizer spreader (solid), sprayer (liquid), manure spreader, slurry tanker

### Step 4: Weeding

- Weeds appear after seeding (visible as small red plants on the field map)
- Use a weeder (mechanical, early stage) or sprayer with herbicide (any stage)
- Weeds reduce yield if ignored

### Step 5: Harvesting

- Crop is ready when the field turns the crop's color (e.g., wheat turns golden)
- Check the growth stage on the map (fully grown = harvestable)
- Use the correct header/attachment for each crop type
- **Direct cutting** for grains: combine + grain header
- **Forage harvesting** for grass/chaff: forage harvester + pickup header

### Step 6: Selling

- Drive harvested crop to a sell point (marked on map)
- Prices vary daily — check the price menu (P key) for best prices
- Store in your silo and wait for high prices

## 5. Crop Types

### Grains (wheat, barley, oats, canola)

- Easiest to start with
- Use a combine harvester with a grain header
- Can be sold directly or processed into flour, oil, etc.

### Root Crops (potatoes, sugar beets)

- Require specialized planters and harvesters
- Higher profit per acre but more expensive equipment
- Best for players with some experience

### Grass

- No replanting needed — grows back after cutting
- Use a mower, tedder (to dry into hay), windrower, and baler
- Feed for animals or sell as bales

### Corn & Sunflowers

- Tall crops, require front attachment for the combine (corn header, sunflower header)
- Corn can also be chopped for silage (high profit)

## 6. Animals

### Types

- **Chickens**: Cheapest entry. Produce eggs. Low maintenance.
- **Sheep**: Produce wool. Need grass/hay.
- **Cows**: Produce milk. Most profitable but most expensive. Need hay, straw, and TMR (Total Mixed Ration = hay + straw + silage).
- **Pigs**: Produce slurry. Eat almost anything (grain, root crops, leftovers).

### Daily Routine

1. Feed animals (fill troughs at the pen)
2. Collect water (if not automatic)
3. Clean pen (remove manure/slurry)
4. Collect produce (eggs, milk, wool)

### Profitability Note

Animals are a long-term investment. The upfront cost is high, and daily care is ongoing. But the passive income from milk/eggs/wool adds up significantly.

## 7. Contracts

Contracts appear at the bottom of the map screen. You can:

- **Do it yourself**: Use your own equipment, get full payment + bonus for completion
- **Rent equipment**: Cost deducted from payment, but equipment is provided
- **Lease out**: Pay AI to do it, get reduced profit

### Tips

- Accept contracts that match your existing equipment (no rental cost = max profit)
- Field work contracts (harvesting, fertilizing) are best early-game
- Transport contracts are fast and easy
- Contracts with the same NPC repeat frequently — good relationship = more contracts

## 8. Economy Basics

### Loans

- Maximum loan increases with farm value
- Interest accrues daily
- Best used for essential equipment purchases early on
- Pay down aggressively once you have steady income

### Leasing vs. Buying

- **Lease**: Pay hourly. Return anytime. Good for one-off jobs (specialized harvesters).
- **Buy**: Full cost upfront. Better for frequently used equipment (tractor, basic implements).

### Repairs

- Vehicles degrade with use
- Repair in the workshop (costs money) or at dealer (more expensive)
- Degraded vehicles lose power and speed

### Seasons (if enabled)

- Crops planted out of season won't grow
- Winter: fields are inactive. Focus on animals, contracts, and maintenance.
- Snow must be cleared if you want to drive on your farm.

## 9. Automation (Without Mods)

You can't install CoursePlay yet — and that's by design. Learning what the AI can and cannot do in vanilla teaches you when automation is actually solving a problem versus when it's just a shortcut.

### AI Workers (H key)

The base game AI is your first automation tool:

- **What it does well**: Straight line field work. Cultivating, seeding, fertilizing, harvesting — if the field is rectangular and the implement is matched to the tractor, the AI handles it.
- **What it does poorly**: Irregular fields, headlands, tight vineyard rows, transport between fields. The AI will miss corners and get stuck on trees.
- **Best practice**: Do the headlands yourself (two passes around the perimeter), then hire the AI for the middle. You handle the precision work; the AI handles the mindless straight-line grinding.

### Multi-Worker Operations

You can hire multiple AI workers simultaneously on different fields:

- One cultivates Field A while you seed Field B
- One harvests while you run a grain cart to the silo (the most profitable two-person operation in the game)
- One fertilizes a completed field while the other sprays herbicide on another

### What You Can't Automate in Wave 0

- Road transport between farm and sell points (AI won't pathfind roads — this is why CoursePlay exists in Wave 1)
- Equipment relocation — you drive between fields, the AI works the field
- Vineyard work — small row widths and trellises confuse the base AI (vineyard CoursePlay support arrives in Wave 1)
- Multi-step chains — the AI won't unload a combine into a trailer, then drive the trailer to the silo, then return

### The Automation Mindset

Every hour you spend driving a straight line is an hour you're not making strategic decisions. As your farm grows, **you should graduate from operator to manager**. Wave 0 forces you to do both. By Wave 1 and 2, the automation mods let you spend your time on the decisions that only you can make: what to plant, when to sell, where to expand, which production chains to invest in. Don't automate the wrong thing — automate the boring thing. Keep the interesting work for yourself.

## 10. Your First Season — A Roadmap

Here's a structured pathway through your first year. Follow this loosely — adapt to prices, weather, and what feels right.

### Spring (March–May)

1. **Week 1**: Buy tractor + cultivator + seeder. Cultivate your smallest field. Seed wheat or barley. Apply first fertilizer stage. Accept a fertilizing contract — same equipment you already own, pure profit.
2. **Week 2**: Crop sprouts. Apply second fertilizer stage. Buy a weeder and remove any weeds. Accept more contracts — focus on ones using equipment you own. Begin saving for your combine harvester.
3. **End of Spring**: All spring crops should be in the ground. If you have multiple fields, stagger planting by 2-3 days so harvest doesn't all arrive at once and overwhelm your equipment.

### Summer (June–August)

1. Your spring wheat/barley is growing. This is your calmest season.
2. **Focus on contracts** — harvesting contracts for AI neighbors pay well and teach you how combines handle before your own crop is ready.
3. **Harvest your spring crop** (likely July). This is your first big payday. Drive it to the sell point if prices are high, or store it in the silo and wait for a price spike. Check prices every morning — they can shift dramatically day to day.
4. **After harvest**: Immediately cultivate and either re-seed (wheat for a fall harvest) or plant a cover crop (grass for winter animals).

### Autumn (September–November)

1. If you planted a second wheat crop in summer, harvest it now.
2. **Plowing check**: If the field says "needs plowing," do it now. Plowing in autumn means you start spring with a ready field.
3. **Equipment maintenance**: Harvest season is hard on machines. Repair everything before winter — degraded equipment in spring costs you planting days.

### Winter (December–February)

1. **Fields go dormant**. This is your management season.
2. **Audit your finances**: How much did you make? What was your biggest expense? What equipment sat unused? Cut waste.
3. **Animals**: If you bought chickens or sheep, this is when they earn their keep (steady income when crops aren't growing).
4. **Plan Wave 1**: You've finished Wave 0. You know how fields work, how the economy breathes, what the AI can and can't do. You're ready for CoursePlay, vineyards, and a new map. Close the books, sell unused equipment, and prepare to leave the homestead behind.

## 11. Production Chains

Basic production chains available in vanilla:

- **Grain → Flour → Bread** (requires bakery building)
- **Canola → Oil** (requires oil mill)
- **Sugar Beets → Sugar** (requires sugar mill)
- **Milk → Cheese / Butter** (requires dairy)
- **Wood → Planks → Furniture** (requires sawmill + carpentry)

Production buildings are bought from the store and placed on your land. They accept raw materials and output processed goods for higher sale prices.
