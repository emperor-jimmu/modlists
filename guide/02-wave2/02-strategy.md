# Expert Strategy for Wave 2

## Campaign Strategy on Legendary

### The First 50 Turns (Survival)
1. **Legendary AI cheats** — AI gets public order and morale bonuses. Plan for it.
2. **No battle saves** — every battle matters. Auto-resolve is more punishing.
3. **Aggressive expansion in the first 20 turns** — cripple your nearest rival before they grow
4. **Accept that you will lose settlements** — plan to retake, not to hold everything

### Mod-Overhauled Systems

#### Diplomacy Overhaul
- The overhaul mod changes negotiation to a offer-demand system
- You must offer something of value for every request
- Trust is a resource — breaking treaties permanently damages relationships
- War declarations require casus belli or heavy diplomatic penalties

#### Economy Overhaul
- Population mechanics: larger cities drain food, generate wealth but require more public order
- Tax rate affects population growth — high taxes slow growth
- Trade route banditry: invest in road infrastructure to protect trade

#### Character Overhaul
- Generals have loyalty factions — they may rebel with their entire army
- Court politics matter: promote talented generals or risk defection
- Family tree management is critical — succession crises can end campaigns

### Endgame Crisis Events
- **Horde armies** spawn from unguarded borders — keep reserve forces
- **Economic collapses** trigger when corruption reaches thresholds — reform down
- **Dilemmas** force choices between short-term gain and long-term stability
- **Rival kingdoms** coordinate against the player — divide and conquer

### Battle Tactics for Legendary
- **Never autoresolve** against equal or greater forces — the calculator is ruthless
- **General sniping** is harder (AI protects generals better) — use cavalry traps
- **Morale management** is key — your units will break faster without your general nearby
- **Terrain is your only advantage** — always fight on favorable ground
- **Withdraw from unwinnable battles** — preserving armies is preserving campaign viability

## Advanced Campaign Mastery (Phase 3)

### Emperor Mechanics

- Once you declare yourself **Emperor** (or another faction does), the game shifts. You gain access to **Imperial Seals** — powerful ancillaries that grant faction-wide bonuses.
- Seals are awarded for achieving prestige milestones. Prioritize prestige generation once you control 60+ territories.
- The **Three Kingdoms** phase triggers when three factions reach King or Emperor status. At this point, the two rival emperors become permanent enemies.

### Vassals vs Direct Conquest

- **Vassals** are useful buffers — they guard borders and contribute armies — but they generate less income than directly owned territories.
- Use vassals for regions with different culture (e.g., Nanman territories) where public order would be a nightmare.
- Direct conquest is better for wealthy commanderies with your culture. Vassalize the poor ones.
- You can **integrate** vassals later, but it takes time and satisfaction penalties. Plan your integration order.

### Satisfaction & Corruption at Scale

- At large empire size (10+ commanderies), **corruption** eats 30-50% of your income if unchecked. Prioritize corruption-reducing reforms and buildings.
- **Satisfaction management** becomes a full-time job. Every turn, check your character list — anyone below 50 satisfaction needs an immediate promotion, ancillary, or marriage.
- Use **Administrators** in every commandery — they reduce corruption and increase income enough to justify their salary.
- The **Prime Minister** position is critical at this stage. Assign your highest-ranking character with "+satisfaction to all" traits.

### Character Skill Trees

- By Phase 3, your core generals should be rank 6+. Plan their skill trees:
  - **Commanders:** Focus on "+satisfaction to all" and army movement range.
  - **Vanguards:** Damage and charge bonuses. They are your field generals.
  - **Champions:** Personal dueling skills and unit buffs.
  - **Strategists:** Ranged damage, formation abilities, and cunning for spy networks.
  - **Sentinels:** Defense, garrison bonuses, and administrator skills.
- Do not waste skill points on redundant abilities. Specialize each general for their role.

### Timing Major Wars

- Do not declare yourself Emperor until you have 3+ elite armies ready. The diplomatic penalty with everyone else is severe — expect coalitions to form against you.
- Break alliances and coalitions **before** declaring war. The trust penalty is worth avoiding the "ally joined enemy" problem.
- Time your wars when your target is already fighting someone else. Let them exhaust their armies, then strike.

### Training Exercises

1. Play a full campaign where you deliberately become Emperor as late as possible while still dominating the map.
2. Run a "tall" campaign (few high-quality commanderies) vs a "wide" campaign (rapid expansion).
3. Master the **Yellow Turban** or **Nanman** factions for completely different playstyles.

**Success criteria:** Reliably win on Hard/Very Hard. Understand *why* the AI makes certain diplomatic moves.

## Battle Mastery & Micro (Phase 4)

### Focus Areas

- **Terrain usage:** Forests block line of sight and reduce missile damage. Hills give morale and damage bonuses. Rivers slow movement and exhaust units. Choke points negate numerical disadvantages.
- **Flanking:** A unit attacking an enemy from the side or rear deals massive morale damage. One flanking cavalry charge can route an entire unit.
- **Cycle charging:** Charge, pull back, charge again. Cavalry that stays in melee loses its charge bonus. Micro your cavalry for maximum impact.
- **Hero duels (Romance mode):** Duels can swing battles instantly. Send your champion against enemy generals but pull them back when outmatched. In Records mode, general bodyguards are powerful but not invincible — use them as heavy cavalry.
- **Strategist abilities:** Fire arrows, smoke screens, and night battles are force multipliers. Use them.
- **Army stances:** March (faster but cannot retreat), Ambush (invisible until attack), Raiding (sustain without supply), and Forced March (extra range but exhausted in battle). Use the right stance for the situation.

### Training Method

1. Replay the same historical battle (e.g., Guandu-style scenarios) multiple times with different army compositions.
2. Practice fighting 1.5x-2x larger armies on the battle map.
3. Watch high-level campaign battle footage and pause to analyze decisions.

## Master Level — Legendary & Optimisation (Phase 5)

### Legendary Difficulty Adjustments

- **No battle saves** — every battle matters. Auto-resolve is more punishing than manual battle.
- **AI morale bonuses** — enemy units take longer to break. Plan for extended fights.
- **AI public order cheats** — the AI expands faster without rebellion. Do not try to out-expand them — out-smart them.
- **Minimal reloading** — embrace mistakes. Legendary is about adaptation, not perfection.

### Min-Maxing Your Empire

- **Character traits:** Recruit and promote characters with "+satisfaction," "-corruption," and "+commerce" traits. Remove or demote characters with negative traits.
- **Ancillaries:** Every character should have a full ancillary loadout. The bonuses compound. Trade for ancillaries in diplomacy if needed.
- **Court synergy:** Certain combinations of court positions + character traits create powerful synergies. For example, a Prime Minister with "+2 satisfaction to all" plus an Heir with "-10% corruption" nearly solves two problems at once.
- **Army specialisation:** By late game, build specialized armies — one for siege, one for field battles, one for rapid response. Don't make every army a generalist.

### Final Challenges

1. Win a Legendary campaign as a hard starting faction (Kong Rong, Liu Biao, or a Yellow Turban).
2. Unify China before turn 100 on Very Hard/Legendary.
3. Play a full campaign with self-imposed restrictions (no spies, no vassals, only one army type, etc.).
