# Wave 1 Strategy Guide

## Efficiency — Working Smarter, Not Harder

Wave 1 is where you stop being a one-person operation and start thinking like a manager. Efficiency isn't about working faster. It's about spending your time on things only you can do.

### The CoursePlay Advantage

CoursePlay (now available in Wave 1) transforms how you use your time:

- **Vineyard operations**: CoursePlay handles mulching, cultivating, and spraying rows — the repetitive passes that would otherwise consume entire sessions. Configure the route once, save it, and replay it every season.
- **Multi-vehicle coordination**: Set a harvester to work a field while a transport tractor runs a grain cart route to the silo. Two machines, one operation, zero babysitting.
- **Saved courses**: Don't rebuild routes every season. Save vineyard row courses, transport paths, and field-work patterns. The initial setup time pays back tenfold over a full growing cycle.
- **WayPoint GPS**: Route your transport vehicles between production buildings and sell points. Click a point on the map and the vehicle drives there. No more watching the road for five minutes per trip.

### Batch Your Operations

- Fertilize and weed all fields in one session, not one field at a time. Swapping implements costs time — minimize tool changes.
- Harvest all ready fields before starting any post-harvest work. The combine stays attached until every field is done.
- Do all maintenance in winter. Don't repair mid-season unless something breaks — scheduled maintenance is cheaper than reactive repairs.

### The Transport Bottleneck

The single biggest efficiency killer in FS25 is transport. Every minute your combine sits idle waiting for a trailer is lost money:

- **Dedicated transport tractor**: Assign one tractor exclusively to grain cart duty during harvest. It never does field work. It just shuttles.
- **Overflow trailers**: Park an extra trailer at the field edge. When the primary grain cart is en route to the silo, the combine unloads into the overflow trailer. Zero combine downtime.
- **Silo proximity**: Place new silos near your largest fields, not near the farmhouse. Transport distance is the variable you control most.

### What NOT to Automate

- **Vineyard harvesting**: Grape harvesters need precision row entry. CoursePlay can handle it, but one wrong turn into a trellis post costs you real repair money. Supervise harvests.
- **Selling decisions**: Never automate when to sell. Prices move. You need human judgment on whether to hold or dump.
- **First pass on a new course**: Always observe a new CoursePlay route for one full loop before walking away. AI pathing surprises are expensive.

## How to Grow Your Business

### The Expansion Flywheel

Your business grows in cycles, not linearly:

1. **Sell processed goods** → generate cash
2. **Invest cash** → buy more production capacity or more fields
3. **More capacity** → more processed goods
4. **More goods** → more cash
5. **Repeat**

The trap: buying equipment you don't need yet. The discipline: every purchase should either increase throughput or reduce a bottleneck.

### When to Expand Production

Add a new production line when:

- Your existing line is running at full capacity and you have surplus raw materials piling up
- You can afford the building *and* the raw material buffer to feed it for one full season
- The new product's profit margin exceeds the interest rate on your loan (if you're borrowing to build)

Don't expand production when:

- You're barely keeping your current buildings fed with raw materials
- You'd need to take a loan exceeding 50% of your current asset value
- The new product requires inputs you don't already grow (buying raw materials kills margin)

### Wine First, Beer Second

Wine has the highest unit value of any product in Wave 1. A batch of aged wine sells for 3–5x the value of the raw grapes. The tradeoff is time — wine must age, which ties up capital in barrels. Start wine production immediately (fill barrels), then plant barley/hops while the wine ages. By the time your barley is ready for harvest, your first wine batch is ready to bottle. The cash from wine funds the brewery expansion.

### Diversification vs. Specialization

- **Specialize early**: In your first year on Le Mechet, focus on grapes and wine. Learn the vineyard workflow. Get good at one thing before you add a second.
- **Diversify for stability**: Once wine production is stable and profitable, add beer. Two revenue streams smooth out price fluctuations. If wine prices dip, beer might be peaking. If both dip, you have contracts as a safety net.
- **Don't add more than two new production lines per wave**: Each production line adds management overhead — more inputs to track, more outputs to transport, more buildings to maintain. Three lines is a business. Six is chaos.

### Reinvestment Ratio

A healthy farm reinvests **60–70% of profits** back into the operation. The remaining 30–40% is your buffer — cash for emergency repairs, price opportunity buys, and the loan principal. If you're reinvesting 100%, one bad harvest wipes you out. If you're reinvesting 20%, you're not growing.

## Expansion — When, Where, and Whether

### When to Buy New Fields

A new field is the highest-leverage purchase you can make — it's the only asset that directly increases your raw material input. Buy when:

- Your existing fields can't supply your production buildings (buildings sit idle waiting for inputs)
- You have the equipment to work the new field *without* buying additional machinery
- The field is adjacent to your existing land (non-adjacent fields add transport cost that compounds every season)
- You can afford the field in cash or with a loan that will be repaid within two harvest cycles

### When to Buy New Equipment

New equipment only makes sense when it removes a constraint:

- **Buy a second tractor** when you're losing planting/harvesting days because one tractor can't be in two places
- **Buy a larger implement** when field work time exceeds the weather window (a 4-hectare field that takes 3 real hours to cultivate needs a wider implement or a second worker)
- **Buy a second trailer** when your combine waits more than 30 seconds for the grain cart to return
- **Lease specialized equipment** used fewer than 10 hours per season (grape harvester attachments, specialized sprayers)

### The Sunk Cost Trap

You already own a small tractor from Wave 0. It was the right purchase then. Now it's underpowered for your new fields. **Sell it.** Don't keep equipment because you paid for it — keep it because you use it. Every piece of machinery sitting idle is capital that could be in a field, a production building, or a loan repayment. The farm is not a museum.

### Map Knowledge Before Expansion

Before buying any new field, do reconnaissance:

- Walk the perimeter. Look for obstacles (trees, buildings, rocks) that will complicate CoursePlay routes.
- Time the drive to your nearest silo and production buildings. If round-trip transport exceeds 3 minutes, that field is a drag on your entire operation.
- Check the field's shape. CoursePlay handles rectangles best. Irregularly shaped fields need more babysitting and generate more wasted overlap passes.
- Know the soil. In Wave 1, Precision Farming adds soil types. A field with poor soil needs more fertilizer and lime — it may not be the bargain it appears to be.

### Signs You're Expanding Too Fast

- You're spending more time managing AI workers than doing actual farm work
- Your loan interest exceeds 10% of your monthly income
- You can't name all your fields from memory
- Production buildings sit idle because you can't grow inputs fast enough to feed everything
- You dread loading the save because there's too much to manage

Expansion is about throughput, not acreage. A small farm running at 100% capacity out-earns a large farm running at 60%. Scale when you have to, not when you can.
