# Modded Gameplay: What Changes and How to Play

This chapter covers the new mechanics, systems, and content added by the modlist across Waves 2 and 3. Each section assumes you have already installed the corresponding mod catalog categories and understand the vanilla mechanics from [02-vanilla-primer.md](02-vanilla-primer.md). If something here references a mod you haven't installed yet, jump back to that category in [03-mod-catalog/](03-mod-catalog/) first.

## Wave 2 — Content Expansion

Wave 2 adds new things to discover, explore, and interact with. The core game loop (farm → mine → craft → build relationships → repeat) stays the same — there's just more of everything.

### Graphics Overhaul (Categories 03–06)

Graphics mods don't change gameplay, but they change how you see the game. The visual overhaul spans four categories:

- **03 — Graphics Base:** Foundational texture replacements that set the art style for the entire world.
- **04 — Graphics Portraits:** Character portrait replacements that give every NPC a more expressive, detailed look.
- **05 — Graphics Terrain:** Map and terrain textures — grass, dirt, water, paths, trees. This is the biggest visual change.
- **06 — Graphics Animals:** Farm animals, pets, wildlife, and monsters get visual upgrades.

**What to expect:** The game looks significantly different from vanilla screenshots you may have seen. Colors may be warmer or more saturated, character expressions may feel more personal, and some locations may look unfamiliar at first. Walk around Pelican Town on Day 1 to re-orient yourself — everything is in the same place, it just looks different.

**Performance note:** Graphics mods are the heaviest category on GPU memory, especially at 4K. If your framerate drops or load times spike, reduce your resolution or selectively disable terrain textures first (they have the largest impact). See [performance/03-mod-impact.md](../performance/03-mod-impact.md) for benchmarks.

### World Expansion (Category 07)

New areas, villages, and locations are added to the map. These may be accessible through previously blocked-off paths, new NPC events, or transport systems you unlock through gameplay.

**What to expect:**

- New areas often introduce **new NPCs** with their own schedules, heart events, and gift preferences. Treat them like any other villager — talk to them, give gifts, watch for heart events.
- Some expanded areas have **new shops** selling unique items. Check these regularly.
- Expanded areas may add **new forage spots, fishing locations, and mining nodes**. Re-explore old routes — the new content is often tucked into places you stopped visiting.
- **Time investment:** Exploring every new area can take an in-game season. Don't rush. The content isn't going anywhere.

**Key mechanic:** Several expansion mods gate areas behind **Community Center progress, heart levels, or seasonal events**. If an area seems inaccessible, you probably haven't met the unlock condition. Keep playing.

### Monsters and Combat (Category 08)

New enemy types appear in the mines, in expanded areas, and potentially on your farm (if using the Wilderness farm map). Combat difficulty scales up compared to vanilla.

**What to expect:**

- New monsters have **unique attack patterns** — don't assume the vanilla dodge-then-swing rhythm works on everything. Watch their telegraphs.
- Some monsters have **elemental resistances or weaknesses**. A sword that melted slimes in vanilla may bounce off a new enemy type. Experiment with weapon types.
- **Boss variants** of existing enemies may appear on deeper mine floors or in special encounters. These hit harder and have more health.
- New monster loot feeds into Wave 2's **Item and Weapons** category — what drops is often the key ingredient for new equipment.

**Tips:**
- Bring more food than you think you need. New monsters hit harder.
- Upgrade your weapon one tier earlier than you would in vanilla.
- If a new enemy one-shots you, you're under-geared. Go back to the previous tier, craft better equipment, and try again.

### Items and Weapons (Category 09)

New weapons, rings, consumables, and crafting materials expand your combat and utility options. This category adds depth without breaking balance — nothing here is a cheat weapon.

**What to expect:**

- New **weapon types** may fill niches the vanilla sword/club/dagger trio doesn't cover.
- New **rings** stack with existing effects. You can wear two rings in vanilla; modded rings may unlock additional slots or combine effects.
- New **consumables** (elixirs, tonics, special foods) give temporary buffs beyond vanilla's food buffs. Buff stacking rules still apply — same-stat buffs overwrite, different stats stack.
- Some new items are **crafted from monster drops** introduced in Category 08. If you can't craft something, you need to hunt the monster that drops its component.

**Tips:**
- Read item tooltips carefully — modded items often have effects that aren't obvious from their icon.
- Sell nothing marked "crafting material" until you know what it builds.
- The Adventurer's Guild may sell new weapons after you defeat certain new monster types.

### NPC Interactions (Category 10)

Expanded dialogue, new heart events, and deeper character arcs for vanilla NPCs. Some mods in this category add entirely new NPCs with full storylines.

**What to expect:**

- Vanilla NPCs have **many more lines of dialogue**. Talking to them daily actually gives new conversations instead of repeats. This matters — you learn more about their personalities and backstories.
- **New heart events** trigger at levels that were previously empty in vanilla. Revisit familiar characters after reaching the next heart threshold.
- New NPCs follow the same rules as vanilla: **talk daily, give loved gifts, trigger heart events, repeat**. Their schedules vary by season and weather — use the same strategies you learned in the vanilla primer.
- Some mods add **platonic and romantic dialogue paths** that diverge based on your choices in heart events. Your responses matter.

**Tips:**
- Don't skip dialogue just because you "know" the character from vanilla — the modded version may surprise you.
- New NPCs have their own gift preferences. Experiment, or check the mod's documentation (not the vanilla wiki).

### Grown-Up Town (Category 11)

Tasteful mature content — expanded dating dialogue, seasonal outfits, suggestive-but-tasteful event scenes, and relationship depth that acknowledges the player character as an adult.

**What to expect:**

- **Dating dialogue** is more mature and varied. Characters express attraction, vulnerability, and intimacy in ways vanilla only hints at.
- **Seasonal outfits** change how NPCs look throughout the year, adding visual variety to daily interactions.
- Some events have **mature themes** (implied intimacy, relationship milestones). Nothing is explicit or pornographic — the guide deliberately excludes that content.

**Tips:**
- This content is optional. If a mod in this category makes you uncomfortable, disable it — nothing else depends on it.
- Most mature content triggers at higher heart levels (6+ hearts), so it won't ambush a new player.

### Music and Audio (Category 15)

New soundtrack tracks, ambient soundscapes, and audio enhancements that replace or supplement vanilla's music.

**What to expect:**

- **Location-specific music** may change. Familiar areas now have new themes.
- **Season-specific tracks** give each season a distinct sonic identity.
- **Event music** (festivals, heart events) may have new compositions.
- Some mods add **ambient sounds** (birds, wind, water) that play alongside music.

**No new mechanics to learn** — just listen and enjoy. If a track isn't to your taste, audio mods can be disabled individually without affecting anything else.

## Wave 3 — Gameplay Overhaul

Wave 3 changes *how* the game plays. Skills work differently, professions have new paths, farming and crafting are expanded, and core balance is shifted. **Do not install Wave 3 mods before you've played extensively with Waves 1 and 2.** These mods assume you know vanilla's numbers well enough to appreciate what changed.

### Gameplay Tweaks (Category 12)

Balance adjustments, quality-of-life features that go deeper than UI changes, and subtle mechanical reworks. This category walks the line between "enhancement" and "overhaul."

**What to expect:**

- **Economy rebalancing:** Crop sell prices, seed costs, and upgrade costs may shift. Don't rely on vanilla wiki numbers for profit calculations.
- **Tool and weapon balance:** Upgrades may have different costs, effects, or requirements. Check the mod documentation before spending resources.
- **Quality-of-life rules:** Some mods adjust how time passes indoors, how long buffs last, or how inventory stacks work. These feel subtle until you go back to vanilla and notice their absence.
- **New game rules:** Some tweaks add entirely new systems — mail-order delivery, crafting from storage chests, or season-specific bonuses.

**Tips:**
- Read every mod's Nexus description before installing. Gameplay tweaks are the category where "I'll figure it out in-game" leads to frustration.
- Test one tweak at a time if you're unsure. These mods rarely conflict, but their combined effect can shift game balance significantly.

### Skills and Professions (Category 13)

Overhauled skill trees, new professions, and sometimes entirely new skills. This category fundamentally changes character progression.

**What to expect:**

- **Expanded skill trees:** Vanilla skills may extend past level 10, with additional profession choices at new thresholds.
- **New skills:** Entirely new skill categories (e.g., cooking, luck, archaeology) may be added alongside the five vanilla skills. These level up through their own activities.
- **Profession reworks:** Vanilla profession choices may be rebalanced, replaced, or supplemented with new options. Don't pick a profession on autopilot — read the new options carefully.
- **Prestige systems:** Some mods add a "prestige" mechanic — after maxing a skill, you can reset it for permanent bonuses and start again.

**Tips:**
- Skill overhauls are the most complex category in the modlist. Before installing, read the mod's documentation thoroughly.
- New skills interact with other Wave 3 mods. A "cooking" skill, for example, ties directly into Farming and Making (Category 14) mods.
- Profession choices in overhauled systems are often **permanent** — there's no Statue of Uncertainty for modded professions. Choose deliberately.

### Farming and Making (Category 14)

Expanded crafting recipes, new cooking systems, additional crops and artisan goods, and deeper farming mechanics.

**What to expect:**

- **New crops** across all seasons with unique growth times, profit curves, and processing options. Don't assume vanilla's "blueberries in summer, cranberries in fall" shorthand still applies.
- **Expanded artisan production:** New machines (distillers, smokers, dryers) process goods in ways vanilla doesn't. Each has its own input/output rules.
- **New cooking recipes** that use modded ingredients from new crops and expanded foraging.
- **Crafting expansions:** New craftable machines, decorative items, and utility objects.
- **Animal products** may have new processing paths (artisan cheeses beyond cheese, flavored honeys, specialty wools).

**Tips:**
- This category adds the most items to learn. Keep a chest labeled "New Stuff" and dump anything unfamiliar into it until you understand its use.
- Processing chains can be longer than vanilla's "fruit → keg → wine." Plan your layout with room for intermediate machines.
- New crops may have **soil or season requirements** vanilla crops don't. Read seed descriptions.

## Wave Progression Checklist

Use this as a rough guide for when to move between waves. There is no strict timer — play at your own pace.

| Milestone | Wave Status |
|-----------|-------------|
| Installed SMAPI and Stardrop, Foundations mods active | Ready for Wave 1 |
| Reached Summer Year 1, comfortable with all five vanilla skills | Wave 1 is working |
| Completed at least 2 Community Center rooms | You know vanilla well enough |
| Reached Year 2, finished the Community Center | Ready for Wave 2 |
| Explored all Wave 2 expansion areas | Wave 2 is working |
| Maxed vanilla skills (level 10 in all five) | You know the game deeply |
| Killed every new monster type from Category 08 | Combat is comfortable |
| Seen new NPC heart events through at least 8 hearts | Relationships are familiar |
| Completed the Community Center or Joja route on a Wave 2 save | Ready for Wave 3 |

**Important:** Wave 3 mods may require a new save. Many skill and profession overhauls don't apply cleanly to existing saves — check each mod's documentation for save-compatibility notes.
