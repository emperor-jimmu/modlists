# Strategy Guide

> **What's your plan for the endgame?** This page covers the big-picture decisions that define every Stellaris campaign. Each wave page prescribes a specific build and path — this guide explains the *theory* behind those choices and how to adapt when your galaxy has other ideas.

---

## 1. Playstyle Archetypes

There is no single "correct" way to play Stellaris. Your empire's ethics, origin, and traditions form a strategy that falls into one of several broad archetypes. Understanding these will help you make consistent decisions across a 200-year campaign.

### Tall vs Wide

| | Tall | Wide |
|---|---|---|
| **Core idea** | Fewer planets, heavily developed | Many planets, rapid expansion |
| **Economy** | Specialization + megastructures | Scale + vassals |
| **Tech** | Fast (fewer systems = lower tech cost) | Slower (more systems = higher tech cost) |
| **Military** | Quality over quantity | Quantity over quality |
| **When to play** | Pacifist, Megacorp, remnant worlds | Militarist, Authoritarian, swarms |
| **When to pivot** | If you're boxed in with few neighbors | If you find a rich cluster of habitable worlds |

**The guide's waves:**
- **Wave 1 (Scion)** — Plays tall with tech focus. Your patron protects you, so you don't need to expand aggressively.
- **Wave 2 (Remnants)** — Starts tall, transitions to wide after first conquest. Relic world ecumenopolis is your economic engine.
- **Wave 3 (Payback)** — Forced wide. You need every planet you can get with 0 guaranteed worlds.

### Peaceful Diplomat vs Aggressive Conqueror

| | Peaceful Diplomat | Aggressive Conqueror |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary resource** | Influence + Unity | Alloys + Fleet power |
| **Expansion method** | Migration treaties, federation, diplomacy | Claims, wars, vassalization |
| **Defense** | Defensive pacts, federations, GC resolutions | Starbase bastions, large standing fleet |
| **Risk** | Allies may not help in a crisis | Everyone rivals you |
| **Victory path** | Federation, Galactic Empire, Score | Domination |

**The guide's waves:**
- **Wave 1** — Peaceful diplomat. Make friends, join/form federations, use your patron's reputation.
- **Wave 2** — Aggressive conqueror. You start militaristic and stay militaristic.
- **Wave 3** — Hybrid. Fight for independence, then pivot to diplomacy to survive the crisis.

### Trade Baron vs Tech Rush

| | Trade Baron | Tech Rush |
|---|---|---|
| **Core resource** | Trade value -> Energy + CG | Research -> Tech advantage |
| **Key buildings** | Commercial zones, trade hubs, gateways | Research labs, Science Nexus |
| **Early game** | Find a trade federation or league | Pile up researchers, Discovery tradition |
| **Mid game** | Spam branch offices, build trade league | Catch up on military tech |
| **Late game** | Trade league fleet + megacorp bonuses | Repeatable techs, crisis weapons |

**The guide's waves:**
- **Wave 1** — Tech rush primary (Fanatic Materialist + Technocracy).
- **Wave 2** — Alloys and economy both matter (fleet + large empire).
- **Wave 3** — Everything matters simultaneously. Tech, alloys, and trade to survive.

### Hybrid Archetypes

Most campaigns blend archetypes. The skill is knowing **when to switch**:

1. **Stage 1 (years 1-30):** Tech rush — build labs, survey, find chokepoints.
2. **Stage 2 (years 30-100):** Expand — claim territory, colonize, build alloy economy.
3. **Stage 3 (years 100-200):** Military buildup — transition to fleet production, prepare for crisis.
4. **Stage 4 (years 200+):** Crisis response — full war economy, mobilize every alloy.

This is the default pacing for all three waves, adjusted for your specific origin and empire.

## 2. The Three Ascension Paths

Around year 2250-2300, you'll pick an **Ascension Path** — a permanent choice that defines your empire's endgame identity. You can only pick one (unless a mod changes this).

When choosing, consider:
- **What does your empire's story want?** Roleplay matters in a narrative sandbox.
- **What does your situation demand?** Behind in tech? Synthetic catches you up. Surrounded by enemies? Psionic shields and jump drives save you. Multiple species to manage? Biological gives you subspecies.
- **What mods are active?** Some mods add content that synergizes with specific paths (Expanded Psionics, Expanded Genetics, etc.).

### Biological Ascension (Evolutionary Mastery)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Required DLC** | Utopia |
| **Required Perk** | Engineered Evolution -> Evolutionary Mastery |
| **Best ethics** | Materialist, Egalitarian, Xenophile |
| **Core mechanic** | Gene-mod your species + subspecies for every environment and role |
| **Key traits** | Erudite (+20% research), Robust (+20% hab + worker output), Nerve Stapled (slavery efficiency), Budding (+0.5 pop assembly) |
| **Mod notes** | Compatible with all mods. Species mods add even more traits. |
| **Best for** | Wide empires with multiple species, multi-climate colonization |

**Wave recommendation:**
- **Wave 1:** Strong fit. Fanatic Egalitarian supports max customization.
- **Wave 2:** Moderate fit. Authoritarian empires create caste systems with specialized subspecies.
- **Wave 3:** Good fit. Materialist, need every edge to survive.

### Psionic Ascension (Mind Over Matter)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Required DLC** | Utopia |
| **Required Perk** | Mind Over Matter -> Transcendence |
| **Best ethics** | Spiritualist, Authoritarian, Xenophobe |
| **Core mechanic** | Enter the Shroud, gain bonuses from covenants, unlock psionic shields/jump drives |
| **Key features** | Shroud covenants (avatars, chosen one, end of the cycle), psionic shields (+50% HP), psionic jump drives (ignore hyperlanes) |
| **Mod notes** | Compatible. Expanded Psionics adds more shroud content. No conflicts. |
| **Best for** | Tall empires, spiritualist roleplay, gambler playstyles |

**When to pick:** Psionic is the "gambler's ascension." The Shroud can give you game-breaking bonuses early (or nothing). The Chosen One event can make a leader immortal. Psionic shields are best-in-slot for defense. You cannot gene-mod or robot-assemble — your growth relies on biological growth + migration.

**Wave recommendation:**
- **Wave 1:** Weak fit. You're Materialist, which conflicts with spiritualist requirements.
- **Wave 2:** Excellent fit. Your Authoritarian + Spiritualist ethics align perfectly.
- **Wave 3:** Moderate fit. Materialist ethics make this harder to unlock, but it's still viable.

### Synthetic Ascension (The Flesh is Weak)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Required DLC** | Utopia |
| **Required Perk** | The Flesh is Weak -> Synthetic Evolution |
| **Best ethics** | Materialist, Fanatic Materialist |
| **Core mechanic** | Convert all biological pops to robots. Leaders become immortal. +200% habitability. |
| **Key features** | Robot leaders live forever, +20% resources from jobs, assimilation of other species, +200% habitability (any planet type), reduced pop upkeep |
| **Mod notes** | Compatible. Some mods (like Gigastructures) add unique interactions with machine empires. |
| **Best for** | Materialist empires, tech rush builds, players who hate managing species |

**When to pick:** Synthetic is the "optimizer's ascension." It gives the most reliable, predictable bonuses. Your entire empire becomes immortal robots with +200% habitability — you can colonize anything. The research speed from erudite + synthetic traits stacks powerfully with repeatable techs. You lose access to biological growth (pop assembly replaces it) and diplomacy penalties with spiritualist empires.

**Wave recommendation:**
- **Wave 1:** Strongest fit. You're Fanatic Materialist. This is the optimal choice for your science victory.
- **Wave 2:** Weak fit. Authoritarian + Spiritualist makes this hard to justify narratively.
- **Wave 3:** Excellent fit. Fanatic Militarist + Materialist. Synthetic troops + immortal generals. The ultimate war machine.

### Ascension Path Decision Matrix

| Your Situation | Recommended Path |
|----------------|-----------------|
| Fanatic Materialist, tech focus | **Synthetic** — peak optimization |
| Spiritualist, tall empire | **Psionic** — shroud bonuses outpace economy |
| Multi-species wide empire | **Biological** — subspecies for every role |
| Authoritarian + multiple planets | **Biological** — caste systems with specialized subspecies |
| Behind in tech, need catch-up | **Synthetic** — research speed bonuses |
| Surrounded by hostiles | **Psionic** — jump drives to escape, shields to survive |
| Roleplay-driven campaign | Pick what fits your story |

## 3. Victory Conditions

Stellaris offers multiple victory paths. Each of the three waves naturally leans toward a specific type, but you can pivot if the galaxy gives you different opportunities.

### Domination Victory

Conquer or vassalize every other empire in the galaxy.

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Best for** | Militarist empires with strong alloy economy |
| **Timeline** | Years 100-250. Continuous war with consolidation breaks. |
| **Key tech** | Ship weapons, armor, shields, starbase upgrades, gateway activation |
| **Key traditions** | Supremacy, Domination, Prosperity |
| **Mod impact** | Warfare mods add ship classes and fleet formations. AI mods make opponents smarter — expect harder fights. |
| **Pitfall** | Overextension. Too many planets = micro nightmare + empire size penalties. Use vassals. |
| **Wave alignment** | **Wave 2** — your militaristic build is built for this. **Wave 3** — your independence war naturally leads here. |

### Federation Victory

Maximize federation cohesion and level, then pass the Federation Victory resolution.

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Best for** | Xenophile/Egalitarian empires with good diplomacy |
| **Timeline** | Years 50-250. Build relationships from the start. |
| **Key tech** | Communications, diplomacy techs, galactic law |
| **Key traditions** | Diplomacy, Harmony, Mercantile (for trade federations) |
| **Mod impact** | AI mods make allies smarter. Diplomacy mods improve federation mechanics. |
| **Pitfall** | Too passive — your allies may drag you into wars. Federation fleet contribution costs alloys. |
| **Wave alignment** | **Wave 1** — your Scion + Egalitarian/Xenophile build. Your patron protects you while you build relationships. |

### Galactic Empire Victory (Galactic Community)

Become the Galactic Emperor by passing the Galactic Empire reform resolution.

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Best for** | Authoritarian empires with strong diplomatic weight |
| **Timeline** | Years 60-200. Join early, build diplomatic weight, pass resolutions. |
| **Key tech** | Galactic Community formation, communications |
| **Key resources** | Diplomatic weight (fleet power + economy + population) |
| **Mod impact** | No major changes. AI mods may make voting more competitive. |
| **Pitfall** | You need fleet power for diplomatic weight — don't neglect your military just because you're going the diplomatic route. |
| **Wave alignment** | **Wave 3** — After liberating yourself, dominate the GC to prevent your former overlord from retaliating. |

### Crisis Victory (Become the Crisis)

Unlock the Become the Crisis ascension perk and progress through its stages to build and fire the Aetherophasic Engine — destroying the galaxy.

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Required DLC** | Nemesis |
| **Best for** | Militarist/Xenophobe empires with strong alloy economy |
| **Timeline** | Years 100-250. Requires steady menace generation. |
| **Key resources** | Menace (earned through warfare, purging, planet cracking) |
| **Key buildings** | Menace buildings, Star-Eaters (planet-cracking ships) |
| **Mod impact** | Some mods add more toys for the Crisis path. Check compatibility. |
| **Pitfall** | Everyone will declare war on you at high menace levels. You need the strongest fleet in the galaxy. |
| **Wave alignment** | Post-curriculum content. Not recommended for learning playthroughs but a natural next challenge after Wave 3. |

### Score Victory

The default victory — highest score at victory year (2500 by default).

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Best for** | Any empire, especially tech/economy focused builds |
| **Timeline** | Continuous. Every decision affects your score. |
| **Key factors** | Population, technology, economy, fleet power, federations, subjects |
| **Mod impact** | No changes. Score is calculated the same way. |
| **Pitfall** | Score victory rewards passive play. You don't have to do anything dramatic to win on score. |
| **Wave alignment** | **Wave 1** — your science victory path naturally wins on score if nothing else triggers. |

## 4. Endgame Crisis Preparation

The end-game crisis is the final exam for every Stellaris campaign. There are four possible crises, each requiring a different counter-strategy.

### Crisis Types

#### The Unbidden

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Trigger** | Researching jump drives repeatedly |
| **Enemy type** | Energy beings. Shields, shield-penetrating weapons. |
| **Weakness** | **Armor-penetrating weapons** (disruptors, arc emitters, cloud lightning). They have zero armor — bypassing shields kills them instantly. |
| **Recommended fleet** | Arc emitter battleships with carrier support (strike craft bypass shields). |
| **Terrain** | They spawn from a rift and expand outward. Rush the rift. Destroying it stops reinforcements. |
| **Wave alignment** | All waves — common crisis, no mod-specific adjustments needed. |

#### The Contingency

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Trigger** | Mid-game year + certain conditions |
| **Enemy type** | AI robots. Armor-heavy, use energy weapons. |
| **Weakness** | **Shield-penetrating weapons + hull damage.** Kinetic weapons are effective against their shields; plasma/energy weapons melt their armor. |
| **Recommended fleet** | Balanced kinetic + energy. Artillery battleships + cruiser screen. |
| **Terrain** | Four machine worlds spawn in the galaxy. Each must be destroyed. A final hub world appears after all four fall. |
| **Special** | Sterilization hubs have system-wide effects. Don't engage without a strong fleet. |
| **Mod impact** | AI mods don't affect the Contingency directly. Gigastructures adds some related content. |

#### The Prethoryn Scourge

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Trigger** | Mid-game year + random event |
| **Enemy type** | Organic swarm. Armor-heavy, use strike craft and missiles. |
| **Weakness** | **Energy weapons + point defense.** Their ships have high armor and no shields. Plasma, lasers, and disruptors are optimal. Point defense destroys their missiles and strike craft. |
| **Recommended fleet** | Plasma battleships + carrier cruisers with point defense. |
| **Terrain** | They arrive from the galactic rim, conquering inward. No central weak point — you must fight system by system. |
| **Special** | Prethoryn can be infected with a special project (research their biology) — this permanently debuffs them. |
| **Mod impact** | No major changes with standard modlist. |

#### Cetana / The Crisis of Aeternum

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Trigger** | New crisis added in 4.4. Requires certain end-game conditions. |
| **Enemy type** | Unique crisis with its own mechanics. |
| **Weakness** | Varies by phase. Requires strong balanced fleet and repeatable techs. |
| **Mod impact** | Some mods may not have updated compatibility for Cetana. Check mod pages. |

### Crisis Preparation Timeline

Regardless of which crisis spawns, follow this economic preparation schedule:

| Years Before Crisis | What to Do |
|--------------------|------------|
| **100+ years** | Build your economy. Specialize planets. Get your ascension path chosen. Build first megastructures. |
| **50-100 years** | Expand alloy production. Build a dedicated forge ecumenopolis or foundry habitat. Begin fleet buildup. |
| **25-50 years** | Fleet to 50% of your target crisis-fighting power. Research crisis-specific weapons. Build gateway network for rapid deployment. |
| **10-25 years** | Max out naval capacity. Build anchorages. Convert all spare industry to alloys. Set trade to militarized economy. |
| **1-10 years** | Fleet at target. Fortify border chokepoints. Starbases with communication jammers, target uplink computers. Rally fleets to a central gateway hub. |
| **During crisis** | Don't overcommit. Fight defensively. Use gateway network to respond to multiple fronts. Coordinate with AI empires (the GC crisis resolutions help). |

### Fleet Power Targets by Wave

| Wave | Crisis Strength | Minimum Fleet Power | Comfortable Fleet Power |
|------|-----------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Wave 1** | 1x | 100K | 150K-200K |
| **Wave 2** | 1x | 150K | 200K-300K |
| **Wave 3** | 2x | 250K | 350K-500K |

### Crisis Preparation Checklist

- [ ] Ascension path chosen and fully unlocked
- [ ] Megastructure(s) operational — Science Nexus, Dyson Sphere, Matter Decompressor
- [ ] Gateway network connecting your core worlds
- [ ] Dedicated forge world(s) for alloy production
- [ ] Starbase bastions at border chokepoints (target uplink + communication jammer)
- [ ] Crisis-specific weapons researched
- [ ] Fleet at or above minimum power target
- [ ] Naval cap maxed out (anchorages, fortresses)
- [ ] Economy positive and stable (don't deficit-spend into a spiral during the crisis)
- [ ] Save game before unpausing — you may need to re-try the crisis fight

## 5. Megastructure Strategy

Megastructures define the late game. They provide resource streams that normal planets can't match.

### Priority Order by Playstyle

| Playstyle | 1st Priority | 2nd Priority | 3rd Priority | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| **Tech Rush** | Science Nexus | Dyson Sphere | Mega Shipyard | Research first, then energy for upkeep, then ship production |
| **Conquest** | Mega Shipyard | Dyson Sphere | Matter Decompressor | Ships first — alloy income + ship build speed |
| **Economic** | Dyson Sphere | Matter Decompressor | Science Nexus | Energy -> minerals -> tech |
| **Tall** | Science Nexus | Dyson Sphere | Ring World | Research + energy + habitable space |
| **Wide** | Mega Shipyard | Matter Decompressor | Strategic Command Center | Ship production + minerals + fleet cap |

### Gigastructural Engineering Mod Integration

With Gigastructural Engineering active, the priority changes significantly:

| Structure | What It Does | Build Priority |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| **Alderson Disk** | Massive energy production + habitable sections | High — but requires late-game tech |
| **System Craft** | Mobile planet-fortress | Medium — niche use for crisis fighting |
| **Maginot World** | Planetary defense fortress | Low — defensive-only |
| **Quadrant Thermo Plant** | Infinite energy from gas giants | Medium — good for energy-hungry empires |
| **Planetcraft** | Gigantic ship that one-shots fleets | Very high — but extremely expensive |

### Build Location Tips

- **Science Nexus**: Build in your home sector. Protects your research investment.
- **Dyson Sphere**: Build in a black hole system (if available) or an isolated system far from borders. They're vulnerable during construction.
- **Matter Decompressor**: Build on a black hole. No choice — it requires one.
- **Mega Shipyard**: Build near your front line (late game) or in your core sector (early game).
- **Ring World**: Build in a system with lots of empty space. Add gates for rapid population movement.

## 6. Tradition & Perk Choices

Traditions shape your empire from the early game to the end. The order you pick them matters more than the specific mix.

### Recommended Order by Playstyle

#### Tech Rush (Wave 1)

1. **Discovery** — Faster research, anomaly discovery chance, survey speed. +1 research alternative.
2. **Expansion** — Cheaper starbases, faster colony development. More pops faster.
3. **Prosperity** — Cheaper buildings, better economy. +10% specialist output.
4. **Supremacy** — Fleet cap, fire rate. You need some defense.
5. **Adaptability** — Habitability, building slots. Helps with diverse planets.
6. **Diplomacy** — Envoys, federation cohesion. Useful for federation victory.
7. **Harmony** — Stability, happiness. Nice bonuses but lowest priority.

**Key ascension perks:** Technological Ascendancy, The Flesh is Weak/Synthetic Evolution, Master Builders, Galactic Wonders, Colossus Project.

#### Conquest (Wave 2)

1. **Supremacy** — Fleet cap, fire rate, ship build speed. War from day one.
2. **Domination** — Stability on conquered worlds, vassal bonuses, influence.
3. **Prosperity** — Alloy production, economy. War needs resources.
4. **Discovery** — You still need tech. Second tradition tree for research stations.
5. **Expansion** — More territory, more pops.
6. **Adaptability** — Habitability for conquered worlds of different climates.
7. **Diplomacy or Harmony** — Situational.

**Key ascension perks:** Defender of the Galaxy, Colossus Project, Master Builders, Galactic Wonders.

#### Survival (Wave 3 — Payback)

1. **Discovery** — Tech advantage essential. You start behind — catch up.
2. **Supremacy** — You must win your independence war.
3. **Prosperity** — Economy after the rebellion.
4. **Domination** — Vassal management, conquered territory.
5. **Expansion** — More planets, more fleet capacity.
6. **Adaptability** — 0 guaranteed worlds means weird planets.
7. **Harmony or Diplomacy** — Your choice.

**Key ascension perks:** Technological Ascendancy, Defender of the Galaxy, Galactic Force Projection (fleet cap when you need it most), Master Builders, Galactic Wonders.

### Mod-Added Traditions

Some mods add new tradition trees. If you're playing with the full Wave 3 modlist, you may encounter:

- **Merged traditions mods** — Combine multiple trees. Read carefully before picking.
- **Expanded tradition mods** — Add new trees with unique bonuses.
- **Gigastructures traditions** — If Gigastructures adds a tradition, it's usually worth taking early for megastructure discounts.

## 7. Pacing Decisions

A Stellaris campaign is a 200-300 year arc. The empires that win know **when** to do things, not just **what** to do.

### The Four Ages of Stellaris

| Age | Years | What Matters | What to Avoid |
|-----|-------|-------------|---------------|
| **Exploration** | 1-30 | Surveying, anomalies, first contact, chokepoints, first colony | War. Don't fight — you have nothing to gain, everything to lose. |
| **Expansion** | 30-100 | Colonization, claims, vassals, starbase network, second/third colony ships | Overextension. Stop expanding when your economy can't support new colonies. |
| **Consolidation** | 100-200 | Specialization, megastructures, ascension completion, tech parity | Stagnation. If you stopped expanding, you must start building. Upgrade buildings. |
| **Crisis** | 200+ | Fleet combat, crisis mechanics, galaxy coordination | Debt. Don't deficit-spend alloys into a spiral. Keep one stable sector producing energy and food. |

### When to Expand vs When to Develop

| Situation | Do This |
|-----------|---------|
| You found a chokepoint system | Expand to it immediately. Claim it. |
| You found a 80%+ habitable planet | Colonize it. Even if it hurts short-term economy. |
| Your economy is negative in any resource | Pause expansion. Fix the deficit first. |
| You're at naval cap | Build more anchorages or fortresses. Don't waste fleet capacity. |
| You have 3+ undeveloped colonies | Stop colonizing. Build districts and buildings on what you have. |
| You're behind in tech (compared to neighbors) | Build research labs on all planets. Discovery tradition if you haven't picked it. |
| You're threatened by a neighbor | Build starbase bastion on the chokepoint. Supremacy tradition. Your fleet should match or exceed theirs. |

### When to Go to War

| Situation | Go to War | Don't Go to War |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Your fleet is stronger | **Yes** — strike before they build up | |
| Your fleet is weaker | | **No** — build up first. Improve relations to buy time. |
| You can vassalize a small neighbor | **Yes** — one quick war for a long-term income boost | |
| You're already in a war | | Finish your current war before starting another. Multi-front wars are a trap. |
| The crisis is coming in 50 years | **Yes** — conquer what you need before the crisis | |
| The crisis is here | | **No** — all resources go to crisis defense. Don't start new wars during a crisis unless it's against the crisis. |

### Pacing by Wave

**Wave 1:** You're protected. Use this to learn the rhythm. Don't rush. Your patron absorbs the cost of your mistakes. Focus on the four ages in order — don't skip to the crisis phase before you've built your economy.

**Wave 2:** You must be aggressive early. Your first conquest needs to happen before year 2300. After that, alternate between consolidation (10-20 years) and war (5-10 years). Don't war constantly — your economy needs recovery time.

**Wave 3:** Your pacing is set by your overlord. Independence war around year 15-40. After freedom, you're in a catch-up phase (years 40-80). Then normal pacing. The early crisis (2375) means you have less time — start megastructures by year 2250 at the latest.

## Quick Reference: Decision Flowchart

```
What's your situation?
├─ Just started (years 1-30)
│  ├─ Survey everything
│  ├─ Claim chokepoints
│  └─ Tech up (Discovery tradition)
├─ Established (years 30-100)
│  ├─ Militarist? → Look for first conquest target
│  ├─ Pacifist/Xenophile? → Build federation, explore
│  └─ Materialist? → Pile on research labs, rush key techs
├─ Strong mid-game (years 100-200)
│  ├─ Pick your ascension path if you haven't
│  ├─ Start first megastructure
│  ├─ Build gateway network
│  └─ Crisis prep: alloys, fleet, starbases
└─ Crisis imminent (years 200+)
   ├─ Identify crisis type
   ├─ Counter-build fleet
   └─ Mobilize galaxy via GC
```
