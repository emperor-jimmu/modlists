# 02 — Vanilla Primer: How to Play Stardew Valley

This chapter covers everything a first-time player needs to know to play Stardew Valley comfortably. The mod catalog later assumes you understand these basics, so read through even if you think you know the game — some 1.6 mechanics may be new to you.

## Welcome to the Valley

Stardew Valley is a farming/life simulation RPG. You inherit your grandfather's overgrown farm in a small town called Pelican Town. What you do with it is up to you: grow crops, raise animals, mine for ore, befriend the locals, fight monsters, fish, cook, craft, and more. There is no wrong way to play, and there is no time limit — the story progresses at your pace.

The core loop: **use energy to do things → earn money → buy upgrades → unlock new things to do → repeat.**

## Character Creation

When you start a new game, you choose:

- **Appearance, name, farm name, and favourite thing.** Favourite thing has a small effect on a late-game event — pick something funny.
- **Farm type.** This determines your farm map layout and gives a bonus to a specific skill:
    - **Standard Farm** — Largest open space. Best for new players.
    - **Riverland Farm** — Islands separated by water. Boosts fishing.
    - **Forest Farm** — Plenty of forage and hardwood stumps that regrow. Boosts foraging.
    - **Hill-Top Farm** — A quarry spawns on the farm. Boosts mining.
    - **Wilderness Farm** — Monsters appear at night. Boosts combat.
    - **Four Corners Farm** — Split into four themed quadrants. Designed for co-op, but fine solo.
    - **Beach Farm** — Large but sprinklers don't work on sand. Boosts fishing and foraging.
    - **Meadowlands Farm (1.6)** — Starts with a chicken coop and two chickens. Boosts farming.

**Recommendation for first-timers:** Standard Farm or Meadowlands Farm. The others add restrictions that make early-game harder.

## The First Day

You arrive at Pelican Town on Spring 1, Year 1. Mayor Lewis greets you, gives you a brief tour, and leaves you at your overgrown farm. You have a small cabin, a few parsnip seeds, and 500 gold.

**Do this on day 1:**

1. Open your inventory and equip the hoe (right-click or X to place in your hands).
2. Hoe 15 tiles of dirt in front of your cabin.
3. Plant the 15 parsnip seeds and water them with your watering can.
4. Walk south into town and introduce yourself. You can explore until 2:00 AM — don't stay up past that or you'll pass out and lose money.

## Understanding the Basics

### Energy and Time

Your energy bar (lightning bolt icon, bottom-right) depletes as you use tools. If it hits zero, you move slowly and can't use tools. Eating food restores energy.

The day runs from 6:00 AM to 2:00 AM. **Go to bed before 2:00 AM** or you collapse and lose up to 10% of your gold (capped at 1,000g). Going to bed early is fine — waking up at 6:00 AM is automatic regardless of when you sleep.

The game saves when you sleep. There is no manual save.

### Tools

You start with five tools:

| Tool             | Use                                               | Upgrade                      |
|------------------|---------------------------------------------------|------------------------------|
| **Hoe**          | Till soil for planting, dig up artifact spots     | Hoe more tiles at once       |
| **Watering Can** | Water crops                                       | Water more tiles at once     |
| **Pickaxe**      | Break rocks, mine ore                             | Break rocks faster           |
| **Axe**          | Chop wood, clear stumps/logs                      | Chop faster, fell big stumps |
| **Sword**        | Fight monsters in the mines (you get it on day 5) | Higher damage                |

Upgrade tools at Clint the blacksmith's shop by paying gold + ore. You must have the previous tier before upgrading to the next. Higher tiers let you charge your tool to affect more tiles (hoe/watering can) or break tougher objects (pickaxe/axe).

### Skills

Every activity levels up a corresponding skill. Each skill has 10 levels. Every 5 levels you choose a **profession** — a permanent perk.

| Skill        | How to Level                     | Key Benefit                   |
|--------------|----------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Farming**  | Harvest crops, pet animals       | More produce, less energy use |
| **Mining**   | Break rocks in the mines         | More ore, gem rewards         |
| **Foraging** | Pick up foraged items, chop wood | More items from wild plants   |
| **Fishing**  | Catch fish                       | Easier fishing minigame       |
| **Combat**   | Kill monsters in the mines       | More damage, better loot      |

## Skills in Detail

### Farming

The main money-maker. Clear some land, hoe it, plant seeds, water daily, harvest when ready. Water is the limiting factor early on — you can only water about 60–80 tiles before running out of energy.

**Crop rules:**

- Every crop has a **growth time** (in days) listed on the seed packet.
- Each season is 28 days. Seasonal crops die when the season changes.
- **Deadline:** If a seed packet says "8 days to grow", plant it by Spring 21 at the latest to harvest before Summer.
- Crops need water every day except on rainy days — the game waters them for you.
- **Sprinklers** (unlocked at Farming level 2) automate watering. They are your top priority milestone.

**Animals** unlock later with barns and coops. Animals produce daily (milk, eggs, wool) and need petting for max happiness.

### Mining

The mines are in the northeast, accessed via the mountain path. Enter any time after day 5 when you receive a letter from the Adventurer's Guild.

- 120 floors, each procedurally generated.
- **Elevator** every 5 floors — use it to skip back to your deepest floor.
- Break rocks for stone, coal, copper, iron, gold, and gems.
- Every 10 floors, the ore tier changes: Copper (1–39), Iron (40–79), Gold (80–119), Iridium (120).
- **Combat tip:** Take food, watch your health, and don't chase bats into crowds. The elevator is your safety net — leave before you die.
- Deeper floors have better ore. Prioritise reaching floor 80+ for consistent gold ore.

### Foraging

Wild plants, berries, and tree seeds spawn across the map. Pick up everything you see. Foraging levels unlock:

- Level 1: Spring onions in the forest south of town
- Level 4: Salmonberries (Spring 15–18) and Blackberries (Fall 8–11) — these bush fruits are great early food
- Level 7: Winter roots and yams can be found by tilling dirt

**Tapping trees** (level 3) lets you place tappers for oak resin, maple syrup, and pine tar — essential for mid-game crafting.

### Fishing

The most challenging skill early on. The fishing minigame has a green bar that you control. Fish move up and down; you keep the bar behind them to fill the progress meter.

- **Tackle** and **bait** make it easier. The Training Rod (buy from Willy) has a larger green bar for beginners.
- Each body of water (ocean, lake, river) has different fish.
- Fish sell for decent money. Fishing in the ocean or mountain lake is most profitable early on.
- **Crab pots** (level 3) are passive income — place them in water, check daily.

### Combat

Fighting takes place in the mines. You get a starter sword from the Adventurer's Guild on day 5.

- **Sword:** Balanced — block with right-click.
- **Club:** Slow but high knockback.
- **Dagger:** Fast but short range.

Kill slimes, bugs, skeletons, and flying serpents. They drop loot you can sell or use for crafting. Levels 5 and 10 let you choose between a fighter and scout path — fighter is better on a first playthrough.

## Money Making

Early game, you need gold for seeds, tool upgrades, and backpack expansion. Best sources in order:

1. **Fishing** — 300–500g per day if you're decent at the minigame.
2. **Crops** — Parsnips are cheap and fast; potatoes and cauliflower scale better. Once you can craft Quality Sprinklers (Farming level 6), farming becomes your main income.
3. **Foraging** — Free money. Spring onions (south of Marnie's ranch) sell for 40g each.
4. **Artisan goods** — Put crops in kegs (Farming level 8) and preserves jars (Farming level 4) for 2–3x the crop value.

**Priority purchases:**

1. Backpack upgrade (2,000g at Pierre's) — triples inventory space. Buy it day 2 or 3.
2. Copper watering can upgrade (2,000g + 5 copper bars) — lets you water 3 tiles at once.
3. Silo (100g, 10 stone, 5 copper bars, 10 clay) — lets you collect hay from grass for winter animals.

## Relationships

Every NPC in Pelican Town has a **heart rating** (0–10, or 0–14 for your future spouse). Give them gifts they like on their birthday for 3x relationship points.

- **Talking** gives a small boost.
- **Gifts they love** give the most — check the wiki or experiment.
- **Gifts they hate** lose hearts.
- **Cutscenes** trigger at certain heart levels. Some are missable if you don't enter the right area on the right day.

Marriage is possible with 6 bachelors and 6 bachelorettes. Reach 8 hearts, give them a bouquet, reach 10 hearts, then give them a Mermaid's Pendant.

**Important:** NPCs have schedules — they move around based on day, weather, and season. If you can't find someone, check their house, the saloon (evenings), or the wiki.

## The Community Center

The central story goal. On Spring 5, you enter the run-down Community Center and find glowing golden scrolls — the Junimos. Repair the building by completing **bundles**: collect specific items (crops, fish, foraged goods, etc.) and place them on the scrolls.

- Completing a bundle rewards you with something useful (hearth upgrade, sprinkler, etc.).
- Completing all bundles in a room unlocks a major reward (e.g., the minecart network, greenhouse).
- Completing **all** bundles restores the Community Center and unlocks the endgame.

**Alternative:** Sell one crop bundle's worth of produce to JojaMart and buy a Joja membership. This turns every bundle into a cash payment (no item hunting). The cost totals ~140,000g. Doing so locks you out of the Community Center route permanently. Pick one on your first playthrough.

**Bundle planning tip:** Keep at least one of every crop, fish, and forage item you find. Put it in a chest and forget about it until you need it for a bundle.

## Early Game Priorities

Your first spring, in rough order:

1. **Day 1–3:** Plant parsnips, buy more as soon as they sell. Buy the backpack upgrade.
2. **Day 5:** Enter the mines. Focus on reaching floor 5 for the elevator.
3. **Week 2:** Upgrade your backpack and watering can. Save 45 salmonberries on Spring 15–18 for infinite early energy.
4. **Week 3:** Upgrade the pickaxe or axe to copper. Push to floor 40+ in the mines.
5. **Week 4:** Make Quality Sprinklers your goal before Summer. Plant at least one of each seasonal crop for the Community Center.
6. **Summer 1:** Massively expand your farm using sprinklers. Blueberries are the best summer crop.

## Seasons and Planning

The year runs Spring → Summer → Fall → Winter. Each season lasts 28 days.

- **Winter:** Crops don't grow outdoors. Use this time for mining, fishing, socialising, and upgrading tools. Foraging in winter (winter roots, yams, crystal fruit) is still profitable.
- **Seasonal prep:** On the 28th of any season, clear your fields. Dead crops don't need to be cleared — they vanish on the 1st.
- **Festivals** happen each season. They're optional but offer unique items and relationship opportunities.
