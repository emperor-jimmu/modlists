# World Content

## Landscape Overhauls

### Core Idea

This subsection owns large worldspace-content changes that make regions feel meaningfully different to cross, rather than just prettier to look at.

The key question is not landscape texture quality, grass rendering, or terrain material fidelity, because those already belong to `modlist-02.md`. The question here is whether `Elder Wilds` should include region-shaping environmental content that changes how specific parts of Skyrim feel as places.

### Suggested Options

- Regional transformation route: use `Whiterun Forest Borealis` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/11343> if the project wants the Whiterun tundra to become a more distinctive boreal-forest region with a stronger wilderness identity.
- Swamp-atmosphere route: use `The Marshlands` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/23062> if the project wants Hjaalmarch around Morthal to feel more claustrophobic, overgrown, and regionally specific through a flooded-forest transformation.
- Rift-identity companion route: use `Aspens Ablaze` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/39998> if the project wants the Rift to read more strongly as a signature autumn region alongside other regional identity mods, while accepting that this choice sits closer to the boundary between world-content shaping and graphics/tree treatment.
- Discipline-first route: keep the landscape-content layer restrained until later section-08 choices are firmer if real playtesting suggests the world already has enough regional identity from the existing graphics, travel, and world-feel stack.

### Complementary Additions

- `The Marshlands` and `Whiterun Forest Borealis` are not mutually exclusive by default because they target different regions and answer different worldspace-identity goals.
- `Aspens Ablaze` can also be treated as a complementary regional flavor layer for the Rift rather than as a replacement for the other two, but it should only be carried forward if the final tree/foliage direction from `modlist-02.md` can absorb it cleanly.
- The practical question for `Elder Wilds` is not just which one wins in isolation, but whether the list wants one transformed signature region, two clearly differentiated regions, or a broader tri-regional identity pass that starts to reshape Skyrim's travel language across multiple holds.

### Recommendation

Start with the discipline-first route long enough to confirm section 08 actually wants regional worldspace transformation instead of just better visuals.

If `Elder Wilds` does want a clear first landscape-content experiment, `The Marshlands` is the strongest first-pass baseline because it gives one region a strong, memorable identity without redefining as much of Skyrim's core travel language as a full Whiterun tundra conversion.

Keep `Whiterun Forest Borealis` as the meaningful comparison if the project wants a more dramatic and far-reaching regional rewrite that changes one of Skyrim's most familiar traversal spaces into something much less vanilla.

Treat `Aspens Ablaze` as the most defensible complementary add-on rather than the primary subsection answer: useful if the list wants the Rift to carry stronger regional identity too, but only if that added overlap with the tree/foliage layer is an intentional choice and not accidental scope drift.

### Why This Is The Right Baseline

Section 08 should add actual places and regional character, not relitigate the graphics stack from `modlist-02.md`. That means the right first step is not another broad visual overhaul, but a question of whether specific regions should gain a stronger worldspace identity through content-like environmental transformation.

`The Marshlands` fits that role well because it makes Hjaalmarch feel like a more distinctive place through an immersive flooded-forest transformation around Morthal and related surroundings, without immediately redefining one of the game's most central open travel basins.

`Whiterun Forest Borealis` is also a real contender, but it is a louder statement because it transforms the tundra into a boreal forest. That makes it a better comparison route than automatic baseline if the project is still deciding how far section 08 should push regional worldspace changes.

`Aspens Ablaze` is worth documenting here as a complementary regional identity layer, but it is also the clearest reminder that this subsection sits near the graphics boundary. It helps if the goal is a stronger Rift signature, but it should stay optional and explicitly coordinated with the section-02 tree and foliage decisions rather than being treated like a free extra.

### Working Guidance For Elder Wilds

- Judge this subsection by real regional travel: walking the Whiterun plains, approaching Morthal and Hjaalmarch, crossing familiar open spaces, and deciding whether the region feels more memorable in a good way or simply less readable.
- Test `The Marshlands` first if the project wants one atmospheric regional transformation that adds world identity without instantly rewriting Skyrim's most central travel language.
- Compare `Whiterun Forest Borealis` if the project wants to push harder on transforming famous vanilla spaces into stronger alternate regional identities.
- Treat `Aspens Ablaze` as a companion experiment only if the project decides the Rift also needs a stronger signature identity and the section-02 tree/foliage stack can support it cleanly.
- Keep this subsection separate from `modlist-02.md` graphics ownership, from section-07 roadside/world-feel tuning, and from later section-08 ownership over cities, settlements, dungeons, quests, and homes.
- Record patch pressure carefully against roads, tree/worldspace edits, city outskirts, lighting, snow, and LOD concerns already called out elsewhere in the repo.

### What Elder Wilds Should Prioritize

- Regional identity that feels like actual new worldspace character rather than just a visual filter.
- A clear distinction between environmental transformation as content and landscape visuals as graphics.
- If complementary additions are used, they should deepen regional contrast across different holds instead of just adding more foliage everywhere.
- Stronger place identity without making core travel routes unreadable or overpatched.
- Enough restraint that later section-08 world-content choices still have room to matter.

### Research Tasks

- Confirm `Whiterun Forest Borealis`, `The Marshlands`, and `Aspens Ablaze` behave cleanly on Steam `1.6.1170` with the current graphics, roads, and worldspace-sensitive stack.
- Test whether `The Marshlands` adds memorable regional identity without making Hjaalmarch feel too gimmicky or overcommitted to one mood.
- Test whether `Whiterun Forest Borealis` earns its broader transformation by improving world character more than it harms readability of Whiterun's iconic travel space.
- Test whether `Aspens Ablaze` works better as a true complementary Rift identity pass or whether it is really just duplicating section-02 foliage ownership.
- Record whether the discipline-first route remains stronger once later cities, settlements, dungeons, and points-of-interest decisions are clearer.
- Track compatibility pressure on roads, outskirts, lighting, tree/worldspace edits, grass, and LOD regeneration work already noted in `modlist-02.md`.

### Risks To Check

- Confusing graphics ambition with actual world-content value.
- Choosing a dramatic regional transformation because it is striking in screenshots rather than because it improves travel and place identity in play.
- Adding complementary regional mods casually until the subsection becomes an unplanned worldspace patch pile.
- Letting this subsection absorb tree/terrain/lighting ownership that already belongs elsewhere.
- Taking on worldspace patch debt too early, before the rest of section 08 is settled.
- Making iconic regions feel less readable or less Skyrim-like than the project actually wants.

### Acceptance Criteria

- `Elder Wilds` has one clear first-pass position on whether section-08 landscape content should stay restrained or include a regional transformation.
- The distinction between landscape content and graphics landscape treatment is explicit.
- At least one strong atmospheric regional route, one broader transformation route, and any complementary regional additions are documented with verified canonical pages and clear ownership caveats.
- The chosen direction preserves room for later cities, settlements, dungeons, points of interest, quests, and homes to be evaluated on their own terms.

## Cities, Towns, And Villages

### Core Idea

This subsection owns actual settlement-content additions and settlement identity work: places that feel more built, more specific, and more worth entering because the settlements themselves gain stronger structure or purpose.

The question here is not whether cities feel slightly denser in a world-feel sense, because section 07 already handled that. The question is whether `Elder Wilds` wants stronger authored town and village content that makes specific settlements feel more like places with their own character.

### Suggested Options

- Broader minor-settlement route: use `The Great Cities - Minor Cities and Towns SSE Edition` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/20272> if the goal is to give Skyrim's minor hold capitals and selected towns a much stronger content and structural identity as a group.
- Modular hold-capital identity route: use `Cities of the North - Dawnstar` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/28952> if the goal is a stronger architectural-identity route for the `Cities of the North` family, where individual minor capitals are rebuilt around distinct regional character rather than generic farmhouse logic.
- Focused single-town route: use `The Great Town of Ivarstead SSE` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/34505> if the goal is to make one settlement feel much more specific and pilgrimage-driven without pretending every town needs to be transformed at once.
- Complementary village route: use `The Great Village of Kynesgrove` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/42639> if the list wants stronger identity for a smaller Eastmarch village without escalating immediately into a whole-region settlement rewrite.
- Complementary village route: use `The Great Village of Mixwater Mill SSE` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/36350> if the list wants a more specific, more believable mill settlement along a normal travel route rather than only larger hold-capital changes.

### Complementary Additions

- `The Great Cities - Minor Cities and Towns SSE Edition` does not automatically rule out more focused follow-up settlement picks if those picks deepen specific places instead of duplicating the same scope.
- `The Great Town of Ivarstead SSE`, `The Great Village of Kynesgrove`, and `The Great Village of Mixwater Mill SSE` are all defensible as complementary additions because they target specific settlements with stronger local identity rather than trying to replace the broad baseline by themselves.
- The real design question is whether `Elder Wilds` wants one broad settlement-content layer plus a few hand-picked memorable places, or whether the broad package already goes far enough on its own.

### Recommendation

Start with the broader minor-settlement route.

`The Great Cities - Minor Cities and Towns SSE Edition` is the strongest first-pass baseline because it gives section 08 a real world-content answer: multiple minor capitals and towns gain stronger built form and clearer identity without forcing the project to jump immediately into an ultra-modular patch story.

Keep `Cities of the North - Dawnstar` as the meaningful comparison if the project wants the more bespoke, high-identity route for minor capitals and is willing to treat that family as a deliberate modular commitment.

Keep `The Great Town of Ivarstead SSE` as the focused comparison if the smarter answer is not a broad settlement package, but a smaller number of carefully chosen town expansions that materially improve specific places.

Treat `The Great Village of Kynesgrove` and `The Great Village of Mixwater Mill SSE` as complementary settlement picks rather than primary subsection answers: both are useful if the project wants a few more villages to feel authored and specific, but neither should replace the broader question of what overall city/town content direction section 08 wants.

### Why This Is The Right Baseline

Section 08 should add actual place-content, not just make settlements feel busier in passing. `The Great Cities - Minor Cities and Towns SSE Edition` fits that role well because it targets the settlements that most often feel underbuilt in vanilla and gives them stronger structure as places to arrive at, move through, and remember.

`Cities of the North - Dawnstar` still matters because it represents a different philosophy: not just fuller minor capitals, but individually distinct architectural identity for the northern hold-capital family. That can be stronger, but it also pulls the subsection toward a more modular and patch-heavy route.

`The Great Town of Ivarstead SSE` is the clearest reminder that focused settlement picks can sometimes do more for world content than trying to transform everything at once. It is narrower, but also easier to justify if the project wants selectively memorable places instead of a total settlement rewrite.

`The Great Village of Kynesgrove` and `The Great Village of Mixwater Mill SSE` are worth documenting because they show the most defensible kind of complementary addition here: smaller settlements that gain stronger purpose and local identity without pretending to become the new core framework for the subsection.

### Working Guidance For Elder Wilds

- Judge this subsection through actual settlement visits: arriving at lesser hold capitals, stopping in small towns on the way to other destinations, and deciding whether a place feels like a real location or just a waypoint.
- Test `The Great Cities - Minor Cities and Towns SSE Edition` first if the broad problem is that too many minor settlements feel thin and forgettable.
- Compare `Cities of the North - Dawnstar` if the project wants a more bespoke identity route for minor capitals and is prepared to treat that family as a modular content direction rather than a one-click answer.
- Compare `The Great Town of Ivarstead SSE` if the list may benefit more from a few carefully chosen settlement upgrades than from a broad package applied everywhere.
- Treat `The Great Village of Kynesgrove` and `The Great Village of Mixwater Mill SSE` as companion experiments if the broad route is chosen but a few specific villages still feel too thin or too forgettable.
- Keep this subsection separate from section-07 city-presence tuning, from graphics-side city/road/tree compatibility concerns in `modlist-02.md`, and from later inns/farms/small-settlements ownership that will handle even smaller place types in the next subsection.
- Record compatibility pressure carefully around roads, outskirts, landscape edits, lighting, snow, and any city-family combinations already documented elsewhere.

### What Elder Wilds Should Prioritize

- Settlements that feel more authored and memorable as places, not just more crowded.
- A clear distinction between broad minor-settlement expansion, modular architectural identity, and focused single-town enhancement.
- Better reasons to stop in towns and villages during ordinary play without turning every settlement decision into a giant patch project.
- If complementary settlement additions are used, they should make specific places more memorable rather than just increase the total number of edits for their own sake.
- Compatibility headroom with the already-sensitive graphics and worldspace stack.
- Enough selectivity that later inns, farms, small settlements, dungeons, points of interest, quests, and homes still have room to matter.

### Research Tasks

- Confirm `The Great Cities - Minor Cities and Towns SSE Edition`, `Cities of the North - Dawnstar`, and `The Great Town of Ivarstead SSE` behave cleanly on Steam `1.6.1170` with the current stack.
- Test whether `The Great Cities - Minor Cities and Towns SSE Edition` gives enough settlement-content uplift to justify itself as the broad baseline.
- Test whether the `Cities of the North` identity route creates a stronger world-content payoff than its likely modular compatibility cost.
- Test whether a focused pick like `The Great Town of Ivarstead SSE` produces more memorable place identity per patch burden than a larger package.
- Confirm `The Great Village of Kynesgrove` and `The Great Village of Mixwater Mill SSE` on Steam `1.6.1170` and test whether they work better as truly complementary additions than as the start of another sprawling settlement-family pile.
- Record what pressure the chosen direction places on later inns/farms/small settlements, dungeons, roadside points of interest, NPC population work, and graphics-side settlement compatibility.

### Risks To Check

- Re-solving the section-07 city-presence problem with heavier settlement overhauls when the real goal is actual place-content.
- Choosing a famous settlement package because it is comprehensive rather than because its added places materially improve normal play.
- Letting modular city-family choices turn this subsection into a patch-collection project too early.
- Adding too many complementary settlement mods until the subsection loses discipline and becomes an accidental collection of every good-looking town page.
- Using focused single-town overhauls so narrowly that the section loses a broader point of view on settlement content.
- Underestimating compatibility pressure with roads, lighting, snow, outskirts, and landscape edits.

### Acceptance Criteria

- `Elder Wilds` has one clearly preferred broad minor-settlement baseline, one modular identity comparison route, and one focused single-settlement comparison.
- The distinction between broader settlement expansion, modular architectural identity, and focused single-place enhancement is explicit.
- Any complementary settlement additions are documented with verified canonical pages and a clear explanation of why they complement rather than replace the main direction.
- The chosen direction improves what towns and villages actually offer as places, not just how large they feel at a glance.
- The subsection preserves room for later inns, farms, small settlements, dungeons, points of interest, quests, and homes to be evaluated on their own terms.

## Inns, Farms, And Small Settlements

### Core Idea

This subsection owns the smaller stopover places that make travel routes and rural Skyrim feel lived-in: inns worth stopping at, hamlets that feel like real communities, and minor settlements or farm-adjacent locations that add texture between the larger city and village content choices above.

The question here is not how to make the big named towns feel more important; the earlier subsection already covered that. The goal here is to decide how much authored content should exist in the quieter places where travel pauses, pilgrims rest, workers live, and the countryside stops feeling like empty space between major destinations.

### Suggested Options

- Small-settlement restoration route: use `Granite Hill` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/14658> if the main goal is to restore or strengthen a small settlement so the world gains another believable stop on the road rather than only larger hold-focused places.
- Travel-stop village route: use `The Great Village of Old Hroldan SSE` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/33189> if the project wants a more authored roadside village with stronger local identity tied to one of Skyrim's most recognizable travel-stop locations.
- Inn-focused route: use `Candlehearth - An Inn Overhaul` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/97542> if the biggest missing piece is that key inns and stopover interiors should feel more memorable and worth using rather than only adding more exterior settlement footprint.
- Complementary village route: use `The Great Town of Karthwasten SSE` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/33032> if the list wants another stronger Reach-side small settlement with clearer local identity along ordinary travel routes.
- Complementary village route: use `Oakwood` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/61007> if the project wants a more distinct small village addition that fills out rural travel space without replacing the subsection's core baseline.

### Complementary Additions

- `The Great Village of Old Hroldan SSE` does not automatically rule out a few additional small-place picks if those additions deepen different routes and regions rather than duplicating the exact same stopover role.
- `The Great Town of Karthwasten SSE` and `Oakwood` are the most defensible complementary village additions here because they broaden the small-settlement layer with different regional identity instead of trying to replace the main travel-stop baseline.
- The real design question is whether `Elder Wilds` wants one strong small-place baseline plus a few memorable rural additions, or whether the baseline alone already gives enough stopover texture.

### Recommendation

Start with the travel-stop village route.

`The Great Village of Old Hroldan SSE` is the strongest first-pass baseline because it gives `Elder Wilds` a clear world-content answer for this smaller-place layer: a recognizable roadside stop gains stronger identity and better travel presence without forcing the subsection to become a broad settlement-restoration bundle.

Keep `Granite Hill` as the meaningful comparison if the project would benefit more from restoring or strengthening another small settlement along normal routes rather than deepening an already famous stopover.

Keep `Candlehearth - An Inn Overhaul` as the narrower inn-focused branch if the real weakness is that key rest stops and tavern interiors feel forgettable rather than that rural settlement content is missing altogether.

Treat `The Great Town of Karthwasten SSE` and `Oakwood` as complementary additions rather than primary subsection answers: both are useful if the project wants a few more memorable rural villages, but neither should replace the broader question of what kind of small-place content section 08 actually needs.

### Why This Is The Right Baseline

This part of section 08 should make the world feel fuller at the scale below cities and named villages. `The Great Village of Old Hroldan SSE` fits that well because it strengthens a memorable travel-stop location and gives the world another authored place that matters when the player is simply moving through Skyrim.

`Granite Hill` still matters because it represents a different answer: restoring or reinforcing a small settlement so the road network feels more inhabited and less interrupted by blank space.

`Candlehearth - An Inn Overhaul` is also valid, but it answers a narrower question about interior stopover quality and tavern identity rather than the broader small-settlement layer.

`The Great Town of Karthwasten SSE` and `Oakwood` are worth documenting because they show the most defensible kind of complementary addition here: extra rural settlements that deepen route variety and regional identity without pretending to become the new core framework for the subsection.

That makes the real distinction clear: roadside village identity, restored small-settlement presence, or inn-focused stopover improvement.

### Working Guidance For Elder Wilds

- Judge this subsection through ordinary travel pauses: stopping for the night, passing through quiet settlements on the way to larger destinations, noticing whether roadside communities feel believable, and deciding whether inns and minor places are memorable enough to support the travel-heavy tone already established in section 07.
- Test `The Great Village of Old Hroldan SSE` first if the project wants the strongest single example of a more authored travel-stop location.
- Compare `Granite Hill` if the world still needs more small-settlement presence along the roads rather than deeper treatment of an already notable stopover.
- Compare `Candlehearth - An Inn Overhaul` only if the real weakness is that important inns feel too plain or too disposable during ordinary play.
- Treat `The Great Town of Karthwasten SSE` and `Oakwood` as companion experiments if the core route is working but the world still needs a few more small villages with stronger identity.
- Keep this subsection separate from the previous cities/towns/villages subsection, from graphics-side settlement compatibility in `modlist-02.md`, and from later quest/home ownership that should remain focused on gameplay-facing content.
- Do not let this subsection become a grab bag of every attractive little settlement page; the point is to choose the kind of smaller-place content that best supports the modlist's travel rhythm.

### What Elder Wilds Should Prioritize

- Better small-place identity along ordinary travel routes, not just bigger headline settlements.
- A clear distinction between restored settlement presence, authored roadside village identity, and inn-focused stopover enhancement.
- Places that make sleeping, pausing, trading, or simply passing through Skyrim feel more grounded and memorable.
- If complementary village additions are used, they should deepen regional travel texture rather than just increase the number of edited settlements.
- Enough discipline that later dungeons, roadside points of interest, quests, and homes still have room to matter on their own.

### Research Tasks

- Confirm `Granite Hill`, `The Great Village of Old Hroldan SSE`, and `Candlehearth - An Inn Overhaul` behave cleanly on Steam `1.6.1170` with the current world-content and section-07 travel stack.
- Test whether `The Great Village of Old Hroldan SSE` gives enough authored travel-stop payoff to justify it as the subsection baseline.
- Test whether `Granite Hill` adds a more meaningful small-settlement layer than another focused village overhaul would.
- Test whether `Candlehearth - An Inn Overhaul` improves stopover quality enough to matter outside pure visual novelty.

- Adding too many complementary villages until the subsection loses discipline and becomes just another list of good-looking rural overhauls.
- Underestimating compatibility pressure with roads, lighting, snow, landscape edits, and adjacent settlement overhauls.

### Acceptance Criteria

- `Elder Wilds` has one clearly preferred travel-stop/small-place baseline, one meaningful small-settlement comparison, and one narrower inn-focused branch.

- Any complementary village additions are documented with verified canonical pages and a clear explanation of why they complement rather than replace the main direction.
- The chosen direction improves the smaller places players actually pass through and use during travel, not just the headline settlements.
- The subsection preserves room for later dungeons, points of interest, quests, and homes to be evaluated on their own terms.

## Dungeons And Ruins

### Core Idea

This subsection owns added dungeon-space and deeper ruin exploration: places that give the player more forgotten corners, stronger delve identity, and more reasons to leave the road without turning section 08 into a pure content-bloat exercise.

The question here is not whether vanilla dungeons should only be prettier or darker. It is whether `Elder Wilds` wants more places to uncover, a stronger underground exploration layer, or a selective pass on existing ruins that makes them feel more substantial.

### Suggested Options

- Broad radiant-dungeon route: use `Forgotten Dungeons (SSE)` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/449> if the goal is to add a large number of new dungeons across Skyrim so general exploration and radiant content have more places to send the player.
- Authored dungeon-pack route: use `Hammet's Dungeon Pack 1 SE` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/12186> if the project wants a larger set of hand-authored dungeon adventures with a more overt custom-content identity.
- Deep-underground route: use `Skyrim Underground SSE` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/131> if the goal is a much stronger hidden-underworld layer with connected subterranean spaces, quests, and a more dramatic exploration commitment.
- Vanilla-ruin enhancement route: use `Dungeons - Revisited` - Nexus: <https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/51798> if the smarter answer is not more new map markers, but making important existing dungeons feel larger, less disposable, and more worth remembering.

### Complementary Additions

- `Dungeons - Revisited` is the clearest complementary option here because it answers a different problem from the new-dungeon mods: improving selected vanilla ruins rather than only adding more locations.
- `Forgotten Dungeons (SSE)` and `Hammet's Dungeon Pack 1 SE` should not be treated as automatic complements just because both add more delves; together they risk turning the subsection into a quantity-first dungeon pile before the rest of section 08 is settled.
- `Skyrim Underground SSE` is best treated as a separate direction rather than an easy add-on because it is large enough to change the exploration identity of the whole list by itself.

### Recommendation

Start with the broad radiant-dungeon route.

`Forgotten Dungeons (SSE)` is the strongest first-pass baseline because it gives `Elder Wilds` a clear world-content payoff without forcing section 08 to revolve around one giant bespoke underground project. It adds a meaningful number of extra places to discover and also fits the travel-and-exploration tone better than a narrower single-mega-mod commitment.

Keep `Hammet's Dungeon Pack 1 SE` as the meaningful comparison if the project wants more authored dungeon-adventure identity and is willing to accept a stronger custom-content footprint.

Keep `Skyrim Underground SSE` as the high-commitment branch if the list wants the world to hide a much larger subterranean layer than vanilla suggests.

Treat `Dungeons - Revisited` as the strongest complementary branch rather than the main answer: it improves dungeon depth and pacing in places the player already visits, but it does not answer the same world-content question as adding more destinations to explore.

### Why This Is The Right Baseline

This part of section 08 should make exploration yield more actual places, not just make existing interiors slightly more impressive. `Forgotten Dungeons (SSE)` fits that role well because it expands the pool of places the player can stumble into during ordinary travel and bounty-style play without demanding that the whole modlist reorient around one highly bespoke dungeon ecosystem.

`Hammet's Dungeon Pack 1 SE` still matters because it represents a different answer: fewer assumptions about vanilla-like world fill, more emphasis on custom dungeon adventures as authored content in their own right.

`Skyrim Underground SSE` is even more distinct. It is not just more caves and ruins; it is a stronger statement that Skyrim should hide a significant underworld beneath ordinary travel space. That can be compelling, but it is also a much larger commitment in tone, scale, and compatibility attention.

`Dungeons - Revisited` is worth documenting because it highlights the other valid route here: maybe the list does not need dozens more locations, and instead needs the dungeons players already enter to feel more substantial. That is useful, but it should remain a companion lens rather than replace the subsection's main new-content decision.

### Working Guidance For Elder Wilds

- Judge this subsection through real exploration loops: taking bounty jobs, following rumors, wandering off roads, entering ruins that were not part of a quest plan, and deciding whether the added dungeon layer creates curiosity or just checklist sprawl.
- Test `Forgotten Dungeons (SSE)` first if the main problem is that Skyrim needs more discoverable places spread across the world.
- Compare `Hammet's Dungeon Pack 1 SE` if the project wants dungeon content that feels more deliberately authored and less like a broad exploration filler layer.
- Compare `Skyrim Underground SSE` only if the list genuinely wants a major subterranean exploration pillar and is ready to treat it as a defining world-content choice.
- Add `Dungeons - Revisited` only if the dungeon layer still feels too shallow after the new-place decision is made, or if testing shows that deeper vanilla ruins matter more than a larger location count.
- Keep this subsection separate from section-02 dungeon visuals, from section-07 travel ambience, from later quests that add narrative-heavy content, and from targeted bugfix ownership in `modlist-15.md`.
- Record compatibility pressure carefully around worldspace entrances, navmesh-adjacent edits, lighting, encounter balance, quest aliases, and any overlap with later points-of-interest or quest-content picks.

### What Elder Wilds Should Prioritize

- More delves that feel like worthwhile exploration, not just more map clutter.
- A clear distinction between broad new-dungeon coverage, authored pack-style adventures, deep-underworld expansion, and selective vanilla-ruin enhancement.
- Dungeon additions that support ordinary travel and discovery rather than hijacking the whole list into one content family.
- If a complementary vanilla-ruin pass is used, it should deepen exploration quality instead of masking an already overstuffed dungeon layer.
- Enough discipline that later points of interest, quests, and homes still have room to matter.

### Research Tasks

- Confirm `Forgotten Dungeons (SSE)`, `Hammet's Dungeon Pack 1 SE`, `Skyrim Underground SSE`, and `Dungeons - Revisited` behave cleanly on Steam `1.6.1170` with the current world-content and travel stack.
- Test whether `Forgotten Dungeons (SSE)` improves real exploration frequency and variety or mostly adds one-time novelty.
- Test whether `Hammet's Dungeon Pack 1 SE` delivers better memorable-delve payoff than the broader radiant-style route.
- Test whether `Skyrim Underground SSE` feels like a compelling hidden layer of the world rather than a separate game bolted under Skyrim.
- Test whether `Dungeons - Revisited` works better as a companion improvement to the chosen dungeon baseline than as a substitute for new places.
- Record patch pressure around entrance placement, quests, lighting, encounter zones, underground space conflicts, and any overlap with later section-08 points-of-interest or quest mods.

### Risks To Check
