# Wave 0 — First Steps into Faerun

*You awaken on the nautiloid with nothing but fragmented memories and a tadpole behind your eye. The world is brutal, unfamiliar, and unforgiving. You are not yet a hero — you are a survivor, learning the rules of a world that does not care whether you live or die. Every conversation could be your last. Every battle is a lesson. Your only advantage: a keener eye for detail and smoother tools than fate intended. This is your origin story.*

## Getting Started

Welcome to Baldur's Gate 3. If you've never played a CRPG or a Dungeons & Dragons game before, don't worry — this guide will walk you through your first playthrough step by step. You don't need to know anything going in. By the end of this guide, you'll understand how to build a character, fight effectively, talk your way out of trouble, and avoid the mistakes most new players make.

### Before You Launch

If you've followed the Installation Guide, you should have BG3 Mod Manager set up with all your Wave 0 mods active. Here's a quick checklist before you start your first game:

1. Open BG3MM and check that all Wave 0 mods are in the Active Mods list on the left.
2. Click *File > Export Order to Game*. This writes your mod list to the game's configuration. If you skip this step, your mods won't load.
3. Launch Baldur's Gate 3. On the main menu, you'll see a *Mod Manager* button — click it. If all your mods appear in the list, you're good to go. If anything is missing, go back to BG3MM and re-export.

Now click *New Game*. You'll appear on a mind flayer nautiloid, a living ship sailing through the Hells. This is the tutorial area — it teaches you the basics safely, and nothing here will kill you permanently if you're curious. Explore every room and loot every container before moving on.

### Your First Controls

- **Movement:** Click anywhere on the ground, and your character walks there. If you installed the WASD mod, you can also hold W/A/S/D to move directly and use the mouse to steer the camera. Try both and see which feels better — you can switch anytime.
- **Camera:** Hold your middle mouse button (scroll wheel) and drag to rotate the view. Scroll the wheel to zoom in and out. Press Home to snap the camera back to your character. If you get disoriented, just tap Home.
- **Interacting:** Left-click on anything that glows or highlights — NPCs, doors, chests, items on the ground. Right-click for a context menu with advanced options like *Examine*, *Throw*, or *Pick Up*. Get in the habit of right-clicking; the game hides useful options there.
- **Save often:** F5 quicksaves. F8 quickloads. Tap F5 before every conversation and every fight. BG3 rewards experimentation and doesn't punish failure harshly — if things go wrong, you can always reload. Treat F5 like a reflex.

**Quick reference for your keyboard:**
- I or Tab — Inventory
- J — Journal (your quest log)
- M — Map
- K — Spellbook
- N — Character Sheet
- O — Toggle Turn-Based Mode (useful for sneaking and setting up ambushes)
- Enter — Same as O

## Character Creation

This is the single most important screen in the game. Everything you choose here — your race, your class, your stats, your background — shapes the next hundred hours. The good news: nothing is permanent. About an hour into the game, you'll meet an NPC called Withers who can respec your character entirely for a small gold fee. So don't agonize — make choices that sound fun, and change them later if they don't click.

### Step 1: Custom or Origin?

The first decision is at the very top of the screen: do you create your own character, or play as one of the pre-written Origin characters?

- **Custom (Tav):** Build your hero from scratch. Choose their race, class, appearance, and background. This is what we recommend for your first playthrough — you'll meet all the Origin characters as companions and experience their stories that way. The world reacts to *your* choices, *your* race, *your* class.
- **Origin Character:** Step into the shoes of Shadowheart, Gale, Astarion, Lae'zel, Wyll, or Karlach. Each has a pre-written backstory, unique dialogue options, and personal quests woven into the main story. Playing as an Origin locks you out of having that character as a companion.
- **The Dark Urge:** A special Origin — you're a custom character, but with a dark, violent past that keeps surfacing through intrusive thoughts and... urges. The Dark Urge is compelling, dramatic, and occasionally horrifying. Save this for a second playthrough — it hits harder when you know what "normal" looks like.

**Our recommendation:** Custom character. Name them whatever you like. You'll meet everyone else along the way.

### Step 2: Pick Your Race

Your race gives you unique abilities that stay relevant from level 1 to level 12. It also affects dialogue — elves are treated differently than tieflings, drow evoke fear and respect, githyanki have deep ties to the story. Pick based on two questions: what sounds fun mechanically, and what fantasy do you want to roleplay?

Here's a quick guide to the races, organized by what they're best at:

**For melee fighters:** Half-Orc. Their Relentless Endurance keeps you alive at 1 HP instead of dying once per long rest, and Savage Attacks adds an extra damage die on critical hits. You hit things, and they stay hit. If Half-Orc doesn't appeal, Dwarf (Gold Dwarf for more HP, Shield Dwarf for armor proficiency) is a sturdy alternative.

**For dexterity and stealth:** Wood Elf or Wood Half-Elf. Both get extra movement speed and Stealth proficiency. Wood Half-Elf also gets shield proficiency and two free skill proficiencies — incredibly versatile. Drow (either subrace) gets Superior Darkvision and free spells at higher levels. Halfling's Lucky trait (reroll any natural 1) is quietly one of the best abilities in the game.

**For spellcasters:** Half-Elf (any subrace) gives you Darkvision, sleep immunity, and extra skills. High Half-Elf adds a free Wizard cantrip — take Fire Bolt for a reliable damage option on any class. Human gives +1 to all stats, making you well-rounded. Gnome gives advantage on mental saving throws (INT, WIS, CHA), making you surprisingly resilient against mind control.

**For unique dialogue experiences:** Githyanki is the most mechanically rich race — Martial Prodigy gives you medium armor, swords, and skill proficiencies you can change daily. You'll also get the most unique dialogue options in the game. Drow evokes strong reactions from nearly every NPC — fear, respect, or outright hostility depending on where you are.

**A note on ability scores:** In Patch 8, racial ability score bonuses are flexible — you can put your +2 and +1 into any stats you want, rather than being locked into what your race traditionally gives. This means you can play a Half-Orc Wizard or a Gnome Barbarian without being mechanically punished. Pick the race whose *other* features appeal to you.

### Step 3: Choose Your Class

Your class is your combat identity. It determines how you fight, what abilities you have, and what role you fill in your party of four. For a first playthrough, we strongly recommend playing a single class to level 12 without multiclassing — every class is powerful and complete on its own, and you'll have a cleaner learning experience.

Here's how to choose, based on the playstyle you want:

**"I want to hit things hard and be simple to play."** Play a Fighter. You get heavy armor, all weapons, Action Surge (take a second full turn once per fight), and extra attacks at levels 5 and 11. At level 3, pick Battle Master — you'll learn combat maneuvers that let you disarm enemies, knock them prone, riposte when they miss, and more. Fighters are straightforward but never boring.

**"I want to blast enemies with magic and control the battlefield."** Play a Wizard. You get the largest spell list in the game and can learn spells from scrolls you find. At level 2, pick Evocation — your Sculpt Spells feature lets you drop Fireballs on top of your melee allies without hurting them. If Wizards feel too fragile (they wear no armor), try a Light Domain Cleric instead — you get the same blasting power (Fireball, Wall of Fire) plus medium armor, a shield, and healing spells.

**"I want to be the party face and talk my way through everything."** Play a Bard. You get Expertise (double proficiency bonus) in Persuasion and Deception, making you nearly impossible to beat in dialogue. In combat, you support your party with Bardic Inspiration (add a bonus die to ally attacks and saves) and control enemies with spells like Tasha's Hideous Laughter and Hold Person. At level 3, College of Swords turns you into a capable melee fighter too. Bards do everything — they just do it with style.

**"I want to smite evil and protect my allies."** Play a Paladin. You're a heavily armored warrior with divine spellcasting. Your signature ability is Divine Smite — spend a spell slot when you hit an enemy to deal massive radiant damage. At level 6, Aura of Protection adds your Charisma modifier to all saving throws for you and nearby allies. This is one of the strongest defensive abilities in the entire game. Oath of Vengeance is the most aggressive Paladin — you get Vow of Enmity, which gives you advantage against one enemy for 10 turns.

**"I want to sneak, steal, and deal massive single-target damage."** Play a Rogue. Your Sneak Attack adds d6s of damage whenever you have advantage or an ally is next to your target. Cunning Action at level 2 lets you Dash, Disengage, or Hide as a bonus action — you're incredibly mobile. At level 3, pick Thief for an extra bonus action, turning you into a dual-wielding damage machine. Rogues also make the best lockpickers and trap disarmers in the game.

**"I want to rage and be unkillable."** Play a Barbarian. Rage halves all physical damage you take and boosts your melee damage. You're the tank — you stand in front, soak hits, and hit back harder. Wildheart (Bear) at level 3 gives you resistance to ALL damage types except psychic while raging. If you want to throw enemies off cliffs, pick Berserker instead — Enraged Throw as a bonus action knocks enemies prone.

There are six other classes (Cleric, Druid, Monk, Ranger, Sorcerer, Warlock) that are all excellent. The ones above are just the most beginner-friendly entry points. Remember: you can respec at any time for 100 gold.

### Step 4: Set Your Ability Scores

These six numbers define everything your character can do. You have 27 points to distribute using the point-buy system (don't roll dice for your first character — point-buy guarantees a balanced build).

| Score | Modifier | What It Means |
|-------|----------|---------------|
| 8 | -1 | Below average — this is your "dump stat" |
| 10 | 0 | Average — you're not bad at this |
| 12 | +1 | Above average |
| 14 | +2 | Skilled — worth investing for secondary stats |
| 16 | +3 | Expert — the highest you can start at |
| 17 | +3 | Expert, but odd — take a half-feat at level 4 to reach 18 |
| 20 | +5 | Peak — the highest you can reach in-game |

Here's what each ability does and how much you should care:

**Strength (STR):** Affects melee attack and damage rolls (unless you're using a finesse weapon, which uses DEX), jump distance, shove success, and carrying capacity. If you're a Barbarian, STR Fighter, or Paladin, put 16-17 here. If you're a caster or rogue, this is your dump stat — leave it at 8.

**Dexterity (DEX):** Affects your Armor Class (if wearing light or medium armor), initiative (how early you act in combat), ranged and finesse weapon attacks, and three crucial skills: Stealth, Sleight of Hand, and Acrobatics. Every character wants at least 14 DEX for the initiative and AC. Rangers, Rogues, Monks, and DEX Fighters want 16-17 here.

**Constitution (CON):** Affects your HP — more CON means more health. Also affects concentration saving throws for spellcasters (when you take damage while concentrating on a spell, you roll CON to maintain it). Every single character, regardless of class, wants at least 14 CON. Tanks (Barbarians, Paladins, Fighters) want 16.

**Intelligence (INT):** Affects Wizard spellcasting and four knowledge skills (Arcana, History, Investigation, Nature, Religion). If you're a Wizard, put 16-17 here. If you're any other class, this is almost always your dump stat — 8 is fine. The one exception: Eldritch Knight Fighters and Arcane Trickster Rogues use INT for their spells, but even they can get by with 10-12.

**Wisdom (WIS):** Affects Cleric, Druid, and Ranger spellcasting. Also affects Perception — the single most-rolled skill in the game (noticing traps, hidden doors, ambushes). And it helps resist mind control spells. If you're a Cleric, Druid, or Ranger, put 16-17 here. Everyone else should aim for at least 10 (you don't want a negative Perception modifier). Monks also use WIS for their AC.

**Charisma (CHA):** Affects Bard, Paladin, Sorcerer, and Warlock spellcasting. More importantly, it affects Persuasion, Deception, and Intimidation — the dialogue skills your main character uses constantly. Even if you're not a CHA caster, don't dump CHA below 10 on your player character — you'll be making a lot of Persuasion checks, and failing them locks you out of content.

**Your beginner starting array:** Put 16 in your class's primary stat, 14 in Constitution, 14 in Dexterity, and distribute the remaining points so nothing important is below 10. For example, a Fighter might go: STR 17, DEX 14, CON 16, INT 8, WIS 10, CHA 8. At level 4, take a half-feat that adds +1 STR (like Heavy Armor Master) to reach 18.

### Step 5: Choose Your Skills and Background

Your background gives you two skill proficiencies and determines how you earn Inspiration — free reroll tokens you can spend when you fail an important check. Pick a background that covers skills your class doesn't give you.

For example, if you're playing a Fighter, your class gives you Athletics and one other skill. Take the Soldier background for Intimidation (which uses your decent Strength — yes, Intimidation can use STR instead of CHA) and another skill. Now you can fight AND threaten people effectively.

If you're playing a Rogue, you already have Sleight of Hand and Stealth. Take the Charlatan background for Deception — now you can lie your way past guards, pickpocket their keys, and sneak into the restricted area.

The Persuasion skill is worth singling out: put it on your main character if at all possible. You'll be the one talking in most conversations, and Persuasion opens more doors (sometimes literally) than any other skill.

## Three Beginner Builds to Get You Started

Here are three complete builds that are simple, effective, and teach core game mechanics. Each is a single class to level 12, needs no complex gear, and comes with a clear game plan.

### Build 1: The Commander — Battle Master Fighter

This build is for players who want to be in the middle of every fight, controlling enemies and dealing damage without needing to manage spell slots or complex resources.

**Create your character:**
- **Race:** Half-Orc. Savage Attacks (extra damage on crits) and Relentless Endurance (cheat death once per long rest) make you a melee monster who refuses to die.
- **Class:** Fighter. At level 1, pick Great Weapon Fighting as your Fighting Style — you'll reroll 1s and 2s on two-handed weapon damage dice.
- **Ability Scores:** STR 17, DEX 14, CON 16, INT 8, WIS 10, CHA 8.
- **Skills:** Athletics (from Fighter), Intimidation (from Soldier background), Perception (from Fighter).

**How to play, level by level:**
- **Level 3:** Pick Battle Master. Your first three maneuvers: Precision Attack (add a d8 to your attack roll when you miss), Riposte (counterattack when an enemy misses you), and Menacing Attack (add d8 damage and frighten the target). You get four Superiority Dice per short rest — use them freely, they come back.
- **Level 4:** Take the Great Weapon Master feat. You'll deal +10 damage per hit but take -5 to your attack roll. Here's the trick: use Precision Attack to offset the penalty. If you miss by 4 or less, add your d8 — now you hit, and you still get the +10 damage.
- **Level 5:** You get Extra Attack — you swing your weapon twice per turn instead of once. This doubles your damage output overnight.
- **Level 6:** Take +2 STR to reach 18 (you started at 17, and by now you should have found the Hag's Hair item in Act 1, which gives +1 to any stat — use it on STR to reach 18 naturally, or take +2 STR here for 19).
- **Level 7-8:** At level 7, pick Trip Attack (knock enemies prone) and Disarming Attack (force weapon drops). At level 8, take Alert for +5 initiative — you'll almost always go first.
- **Level 11:** Improved Extra Attack gives you THREE attacks per action. With Action Surge, that's six attacks in one turn. With Great Weapon Master active, that's up to 60 bonus damage just from the feat, plus your weapon damage and STR modifier on every hit.

**Your gear priorities:** The biggest two-handed weapon you can find (greatsword, greataxe, maul). Heavy armor. The Everburn Blade on the nautiloid is your first upgrade — have Shadowheart cast Command: Drop on Commander Zhalk to get it. Later, look for the Sword of Justice (Act 1, tollhouse) and eventually Balduran's Giantslayer (Act 3).

**Your party:** You're the frontline and primary damage dealer. Bring a support caster (Shadowheart or respecced Gale), a lockpicker (Astarion), and a ranged damage dealer or controller to round out the group.

### Build 2: The Burning Light — Light Domain Cleric

This build is for players who want to heal allies, buff the party, and incinerate enemies with holy fire — all on one character.

**Create your character:**
- **Race:** Wood Half-Elf. Extra movement speed, Stealth proficiency, and shield proficiency make you surprisingly durable for a caster.
- **Class:** Cleric. At level 1, choose Light Domain — this gives you Burning Hands and Faerie Fire as always-prepared spells, plus Warding Flare (reaction: impose disadvantage on an enemy's attack against you).
- **Ability Scores:** STR 8, DEX 14, CON 16, INT 10, WIS 17, CHA 8.
- **Cantrips:** Guidance (add d4 to ALL skill checks out of combat — cast it before every dialogue and every lockpick), Sacred Flame (your main damage cantrip — radiant damage, DEX save), and Thaumaturgy (gives advantage on Intimidation and Performance checks).

**How to play, level by level:**
- **Level 1-2:** Your combat loop is simple: cast Bless (the best level 1 spell in the game — adds d4 to attack rolls and saving throws for three allies) on your first turn. Then use Sacred Flame for damage. If enemies cluster up, use Burning Hands. Use Warding Flare every turn you're targeted — it's a reaction, it doesn't cost your action.
- **Level 2:** Radiance of the Dawn is your Channel Divinity — a massive AoE radiant damage burst that destroys undead and clears magical darkness. Use it when enemies group up.
- **Level 3-4:** You get Scorching Ray (three beams of fire, excellent for finishing wounded enemies) and Flaming Sphere (a rolling ball of fire you can move as a bonus action). At level 4, take +2 WIS to reach 18 (use the Hag's Hair in Act 1 for +1 WIS, then +1 WIS from a half-feat — or just take +2 WIS for 19).
- **Level 5:** This is where you become terrifying. You get Fireball — the iconic AoE nuke. You also get Spirit Guardians — a radiant damage aura that follows you and damages every enemy you walk near. Cast Spirit Guardians, then spend every subsequent turn using your action for Sacred Flame/Fireball and your movement to walk through enemies. You become a radiant lawnmower.
- **Level 8:** Take War Caster. You're concentrating on Spirit Guardians or Bless in every fight, and War Caster gives you advantage on concentration saves. You'll almost never lose your concentration.
- **Level 12:** Take Resilient: Constitution. Combined with War Caster, your concentration is nearly unbreakable.

**Your gear priorities:** Medium armor + shield. Luminous Armor (found in the Underdark, Act 1) is your best-in-slot for the entire game — it radiates a Shockwave every time you deal radiant damage, stacking Radiant Orb on enemies (each stack gives -1 to attack rolls). With Spirit Guardians active, you'll stack Radiant Orb on every enemy near you every turn — they'll have -8 or more to hit and basically never land an attack.

**Your party:** You're the healer, buffer, and AoE blaster. You free up a slot that would normally go to a dedicated healer. Bring a melee damage dealer, a lockpicker, and a fourth flex slot (another damage dealer or a controller).

### Build 3: The Silver Tongue — College of Swords Bard

This build is for players who want to do everything — fight in melee, cast spells, pick locks, talk through any situation, and be the main character in every sense. The Bard is the ultimate "face" class.

**Create your character:**
- **Race:** Drow. Superior Darkvision, free Faerie Fire and Darkness spells at level 3/5, and proficiency with rapiers and hand crossbows. NPCs react strongly to drow — some with fear, some with respect. Your dialogue options are richer.
- **Class:** Bard. You get three skills of your choice plus any three instrument proficiencies (pick whatever sounds fun — they're all cosmetic).
- **Ability Scores:** STR 8, DEX 17, CON 14, INT 8, WIS 10, CHA 16.
- **Skills:** From Bard: Persuasion, Sleight of Hand, Perception. From Charlatan background: Deception. From your remaining pick: Acrobatics or Insight.

**Your spell toolkit (level 1-2):**
- Vicious Mockery (cantrip): You literally insult enemies to death. Psychic damage, and the target gets disadvantage on their next attack roll. The voice lines are darkly hilarious.
- Dissonant Whispers (level 1): Psychic damage + the target must run away from you, triggering opportunity attacks from your melee allies. This is one of the most efficient damage spells in the game when you have a melee-heavy party.
- Healing Word (level 1): Bonus action, ranged heal. This is the only healing spell you need — it picks up downed allies from anywhere. Never use your action to heal; always use Healing Word as a bonus action.
- Faerie Fire (from Drow): AoE advantage. Every enemy in the area must save or be outlined in light, giving your entire party advantage on attacks against them.
- Tasha's Hideous Laughter (level 1): Single-target hard crowd control. The target falls prone and can't act. Great for removing the scariest enemy from the fight while you clean up the rest.

**How to play, level by level:**
- **Level 3:** College of Swords. Pick Two-Weapon Fighting as your Fighting Style (add DEX to off-hand damage). You get Blade Flourishes — spend Bardic Inspiration on your weapon attacks for bonus effects. Slashing Flourish hits two enemies with one attack. Defensive Flourish adds your inspiration die to your AC for a turn. Mobile Flourish pushes an enemy and teleports after them.
- **Level 4:** Take +2 DEX (or +1 DEX if you got Hag's Hair). You want 18-20 DEX by mid-game.
- **Level 5:** Bardic Inspiration recharges on short rests instead of long rests. This is enormous — you can use Blade Flourishes in every fight. Your Font of Inspiration also gives d8 inspiration dice instead of d6.
- **Level 6:** Extra Attack. You now attack twice per action, applying Blade Flourishes to both hits.
- **Level 8:** Take Sharpshooter if you prefer ranged combat (dual hand crossbows + Sharpshooter is devastating), or +2 CHA if you want your spells to land more reliably.
- **Level 10:** Magical Secrets. Pick Counterspell (shut down enemy casters) and either Spirit Guardians (radiant AoE aura) or Banishing Smite (massive single-target damage). This is when you become a true hybrid.
- **Level 12:** Alert or War Caster, depending on whether you need initiative or concentration protection.

**Your gear priorities:** Rapier + shield or dual hand crossbows. Light armor. Your two most important items: Helm of Arcane Acuity (Act 2, Mason's Guild — weapon attacks add +2 spell save DC per hit, stacking up to +10) and Band of the Mystic Scoundrel (Act 3 — after hitting with a weapon, you can cast an illusion or enchantment spell as a bonus action). With these items, your combat loop becomes: attack twice (building Arcane Acuity stacks to +8 DC), then bonus action cast Hold Person/Monster with an unbeatable save DC. The target is paralyzed; your next attacks are guaranteed critical hits.

**Your party:** You're the face, the lockpicker, the secondary damage dealer, and the support caster. With this Bard, you don't need Astarion (you handle locks), you don't need a dedicated healer (Healing Word is enough), and you don't need a dedicated party face (you ARE the party face). This frees up your remaining three slots for pure damage and control.

## The Mechanics That Actually Matter

Here are the systems you'll interact with every session. Understanding these will take you from confused to confident.

### Actions, Bonus Actions, and Reactions

Every character gets one of each per turn in combat. Understanding what you can do with each is the core tactical skill of BG3.

**Your Action** is your "main thing." For a Fighter, it's attacking with your weapon. For a Wizard, it's casting Fireball. For anyone, it can also be: Dash (double movement), Disengage (escape melee without provoking attacks), Hide (become invisible if you break line of sight), Help (revive a downed ally or give advantage to an ally's next attack), or Shove (push an enemy, potentially off a cliff).

**Your Bonus Action** is your "extra thing." Not every character has a bonus action every turn, but you should try to use it when you can. Common bonus actions: off-hand weapon attack (dual-wielders), drinking a potion, jumping, shoving (for some builds), or class-specific abilities like a Rogue's Cunning Action or a Cleric's Healing Word.

**Your Reaction** triggers automatically when its condition is met. The most common is the Opportunity Attack — if an enemy leaves your melee range, you get a free swing. Reactions refresh at the start of your turn. Some powerful reactions include Counterspell (cancel an enemy's spell), Shield (+5 AC for a turn), and Warding Flare (impose disadvantage on an incoming attack).

**The golden rule:** You can't use an Action as a Bonus Action or vice versa. If you've used your Action and have nothing for your Bonus Action... just Jump! It gives free mobility and costs nothing.

### The Rest System: Short Rests and Long Rests

New players often try to minimize resting because other games punish it. BG3 does the opposite — resting advances companion storylines, triggers romance scenes, and moves the plot forward. You should rest *frequently*.

**Short Rest** (2 per long rest): Heals half your HP. Restores Warlock spell slots, Fighter's Action Surge and Second Wind, Monk's Ki, and Bardic Inspiration (from level 5+). Takes no resources. Use after every 1-2 fights.

**Long Rest:** Fully heals your party and restores ALL resources — spell slots, class abilities, everything. Costs 40 Camp Supplies. More importantly, long rests trigger camp events — companion conversations, romance progression, quest revelations. If you go more than 3-4 fights without long resting, you're probably missing character development.

**Camp Supplies:** Food is everywhere. Barrels, crates, tables, corpses — the game throws food at you constantly. Right-click any food and select *Send to Camp* to store it. You'll have more supplies than you can use by mid-Act 1. Don't hoard them — use them.

### Inspiration: Your Free Rerolls

Inspiration is a reroll token. You earn it by acting in character — a Soldier earns Inspiration for martial prowess, a Charlatan for successful cons, a Sage for discovering ancient lore. You can hold up to 4 points.

When you fail an important dialogue check, the game offers to spend an Inspiration point for a reroll. Use these on critical story moments — convincing a boss, detecting a deadly trap, persuading a companion to trust you. Don't waste them on minor checks. You can see your current points as small diamond icons next to the minimap.

### Dialogue Checks and Why Failing is Fine

During conversations, you'll see bracketed options: [Persuasion], [Deception], [Arcana], [Insight], and more. These are ability checks using your character's skills. The number in the bracket is the DC (difficulty class) — the total you need to roll.

Before attempting a hard check, stack buffs: cast Guidance (+d4), use Bardic Inspiration (+d6/d8), or drink an Elixir of Heroism (+d4 to all checks). If you have Enhance Ability cast on you, you get advantage (roll twice, take the higher).

**Here's the most important thing:** failing a dialogue check is not game over. The game is designed for failure. A failed Persuasion check doesn't end the quest — it opens a different path. A failed Deception starts a fight you could have avoided — but that fight is content you would have missed otherwise. Some of the best moments in BG3 come from failure. Let the dice tell a story.

### Concentration: The Invisible Rule That Controls Casters

Many powerful spells — Bless, Haste, Spirit Guardians, Hold Person, Hunger of Hadar — require concentration. You can only concentrate on ONE spell at a time. Casting a second concentration spell immediately ends the first.

When you take damage while concentrating, you roll a Constitution saving throw (DC = 10 or half the damage taken, whichever is higher). If you fail, the spell ends. This is why War Caster and Resilient: Constitution are so valuable on casters — they make your concentration nearly unbreakable.

**If you're concentrating on something important:** stay out of melee range, use cover, and consider taking the Dodge action if you're being targeted. Losing concentration on Haste is particularly brutal — the hasted character becomes lethargic and skips their next turn.

### Advantage and Disadvantage

Advantage means rolling two d20s and taking the higher result (worth about +4-5 on average). Disadvantage means rolling two and taking the lower. They cancel each other out — if you have both advantage and disadvantage, you roll normally, no matter how many sources of each exist.

**How to get advantage:** attack from stealth, attack a prone/blinded/paralyzed enemy, attack from high ground with ranged weapons, use Faerie Fire, Guiding Bolt, or a Barbarian's Reckless Attack. Pro tip: knocking an enemy prone with a Battle Master's Trip Attack gives all your melee allies advantage against them.

## Combat Basics That Win Fights

### Position Matters More Than Stats

**High ground:** Ranged attacks from above get +2 to attack rolls (equivalent to a 10% accuracy boost). If there's a ledge, a balcony, a rooftop — get on it. Every time. Enemies on high ground don't suffer any penalty when attacking down at you, so deny them the high ground when you can.

**Don't stand next to enemies as a ranged character:** If you're holding a bow and an enemy is in your face, your ranged attacks have disadvantage. Casters should use Shocking Grasp (melee cantrip) or Disengage before casting ranged spells.

**Opportunity Attacks:** If you walk away from an enemy without using Disengage, they get a free attack. Rogues and Monks can Disengage as a bonus action. Everyone else: either kill the enemy first, shove them away, or tank the hit.

### Surfaces Change Everything

The ground itself is a weapon in BG3. Here are the combos worth knowing:

- **Create Water + Lightning spell = double damage.** The wet condition makes targets vulnerable to lightning and cold (2x damage). Cast Create Water (level 1) on a group of enemies, then hit them with a Lightning Bolt or Witch Bolt. The water surface also becomes electrified, dealing ongoing damage and potentially stunning.
- **Grease + Fire = Burning surface.** Throw a grease bottle at a choke point, enemies slip and fall prone. Then Fire Bolt the grease — it ignites, dealing fire damage to everyone standing in it.
- **Ice + Fire = Water + Lightning.** An ice surface from Ice Storm or Chromatic Orb: Ice melts when hit by fire, creating water. That water can then be electrified. Three surfaces, three damage types, one turn of setup.

### Shove, Jump, Throw — Don't Ignore These

These are bonus actions available to everyone. Beginners ignore them. Experienced players use them every turn.

- **Shove:** Push enemies 1-5 meters based on your Athletics skill. Pushing enemies off cliffs, into chasms, into lava, into your Hunger of Hadar zone — this is one of the most fun and effective tactics in BG3. A single shove can kill an enemy at full HP if there's a bottomless pit nearby.
- **Jump:** Leap to reposition. Jumping doesn't provoke opportunity attacks, so you can jump out of melee range safely. STR characters jump further — a 20 STR Barbarian can leap across entire rooms.
- **Throw:** Throw weapons, items, potions, or even small enemies. Throw a healing potion at a downed ally to revive them from range. Throw a grease bottle to set up a combo. If you're an Eldritch Knight Fighter with Weapon Bond, your thrown weapon returns to your hand. The Tavern Brawler feat doubles your STR modifier on thrown attacks, making throw builds some of the highest DPS in the game.

## Your First Few Hours: Practical Advice

### Who to Recruit First

After the nautiloid crashes, you're on a beach. Here's the optimal order to find your companions:

1. **Shadowheart** — She's unconscious on the beach right after the crash. You literally can't miss her. She's a Cleric — your healer for the early game.
2. **Astarion** — West of the crashed nautiloid, near the cliff path. He'll ask for help with a "monster." Talk to him. He's a Rogue — your lockpicker and trap disarmer.
3. **Gale** — At the Roadside Cliffs waypoint, there's a glowing purple sigil on a rock wall. Interact with the malfunctioning portal. He's a Wizard — your arcane caster and source of AoE damage.
4. **Lae'zel** — North of the Roadside Cliffs. You'll find her trapped by two tieflings. She's a Fighter — your frontline damage dealer. If you miss her here, she shows up later near the Mountain Pass.
5. **Wyll** — Inside the Druid Grove, training tiefling children. Talk to him after the goblin attack at the gate. He's a Warlock — a spellcaster who recovers slots on short rests.
6. **Karlach** — East of the Risen Road, along the river. Follow the trail of blood and burned ground. Bring Wyll for unique dialogue. She's a Barbarian — your tank and melee powerhouse.

### On the Nautiloid: One Thing Worth Doing

Before you leave the tutorial ship, in the final room, Commander Zhalk (the cambion fighting the mind flayer) drops the **Everburn Blade** — a greatsword that deals extra fire damage and ignores fire resistance. It's the best weapon for the first 5+ hours of the game.

To get it: have Shadowheart cast Command: Drop on Zhalk. He'll fail eventually (his wisdom save isn't great). Save before attempting — the fight is challenging, but the reward is worth it. Grab the sword and run to the transponder. You don't need to kill Zhalk.

### Party Composition Basics

A balanced four-person party covers these roles:

1. **The Face:** Your main character, ideally with Persuasion proficiency. You'll be in every conversation.
2. **The Lockpicker:** Someone with high DEX and Sleight of Hand. Astarion, or any Rogue/Bard.
3. **Support/Healer:** Someone with healing spells and buffs. Shadowheart (or Withers-respecced Gale).
4. **Flex/Damage:** Your fourth slot. Bring whatever you need — another damage dealer, a controller, or a tank.

There's no "wrong" composition. BG3 can be beaten with any party. But having a face, a lockpicker, and a healer removes 90% of the friction new players encounter.

### Three Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Hoarding consumables.** You will find more potions, scrolls, arrows, and elixirs than you could ever use. Use them. Drink that Potion of Speed before a tough fight. Fire that Arrow of Many Targets into a crowd. Throw that Grease Bottle. The game gives you hundreds of consumables specifically so you'll use them.

2. **Minimizing long rests.** As we covered above: rest often. The story advances during camp scenes. If you've done 3+ fights, long rest. Your companions will thank you with character development.

3. **Save-scumming every failed check.** Let some failures stick. The game's reactivity to failure is one of its greatest strengths. You'll see content that perfectionist players never experience. And remember: Withers can respec you for 100g. Build mistakes are cheap. Embrace the chaos.
