# Wave 0 — First Steps into Faerun

*You awaken on the nautiloid with nothing but fragmented memories and a tadpole behind your eye. The world is brutal, unfamiliar, and unforgiving. You are not yet a hero — you are a survivor, learning the rules of a world that does not care whether you live or die. Every conversation could be your last. Every battle is a lesson. Your only advantage: a keener eye for detail and smoother tools than fate intended. This is your origin story.*

## Getting Started

Welcome to Baldur's Gate 3. If you're new to CRPGs or Dungeons & Dragons, don't worry — this guide will walk you through everything you need to know for a smooth first playthrough.

### Controls

- **Movement:** Click anywhere on the ground to move your party. With the WASD Character Movement mod installed (see Wave 0 Modlist), you can also use WASD keys for direct character control and mouse to steer the camera.
- **Camera:** Hold middle mouse button or right-click and drag to rotate. Scroll wheel zooms in and out. Press Home to snap back to your character.
- **Interaction:** Left-click on objects, NPCs, or items to interact. Right-click for a context menu with more options (examine, pick up, throw, etc.).
- **Quick Save / Quick Load:** F5 to quicksave, F8 to quickload. Use these liberally — BG3 rewards experimentation and curiosity, and you can always reload if things go sideways.
- **Key Menus:**
  - I or Tab — Inventory
  - J — Journal (quest log)
  - M — Map
  - K — Spellbook
  - N — Character Sheet
  - O — Turn-Based Mode toggle
  - Enter — Toggle turn-based mode

### First Launch

When you launch the game with mods installed, the main menu will show a "Mod Manager" option. Click it to verify all your Wave 0 mods are listed as active. If anything is missing, re-check BG3MM (see the Installation Guide).

Start a new game. You'll begin on a mind flayer nautiloid — a tutorial area that teaches basic controls. Explore thoroughly before proceeding; there are useful items and a powerful weapon if you're bold.

## Character Creation

This is the most important screen in the game. Your choices here determine your playstyle, roleplay options, and how the world reacts to you for the entire 100+ hour journey.

### Custom Character vs. Origin Character

You have two options at the top of character creation:

- **Custom Character (Tav):** Create your own hero. Choose race, class, appearance, and background. Recommended for first playthrough — you experience the story as *your* character.
- **Origin Character:** Play as one of the companion characters (Shadowheart, Gale, Astarion, Lae'zel, Wyll, Karlach, or The Dark Urge). Each has a pre-written backstory and unique dialogue. The Dark Urge is a custom-origin hybrid with a dark, violent past — compelling but best saved for a second playthrough.

**Recommendation:** Custom character for your first run. You'll meet all the origin characters as companions and experience their stories that way.

### Races

Each race grants unique traits and abilities. Your race also affects dialogue — NPCs react differently to a drow than a halfling, for example.

**Elf:** Proficient with longswords/shortswords/longbows/shortbows. Darkvision. Immune to magical sleep. Subraces:
- *High Elf:* One free Wizard cantrip (pick Fire Bolt for a damage option on any class).
- *Wood Elf:* Extra movement speed and Stealth proficiency. Excellent for stealthy characters.

**Drow:** Superior Darkvision (24m). Free spells at levels 3 and 5 (Faerie Fire, Darkness). NPCs react strongly to drow — fear, respect, or hostility. Subraces:
- *Seldarine Drow:* Surface drow who rejected Lolth's cruelty.
- *Lolth-Sworn Drow:* Underdark drow loyal to the spider goddess. More hostile reactions from surface NPCs.

**Human:** +1 to all ability scores. Proficiency with shields, spears, pikes, halberds, and light armor. The most versatile and well-rounded race.

**Half-Elf:** Darkvision. Fey Ancestry (sleep immunity, advantage vs. charm). Proficiency in any two skills of your choice. Subraces:
- *High Half-Elf:* One free Wizard cantrip.
- *Wood Half-Elf:* Extra movement speed and Stealth.
- *Drow Half-Elf:* Free Faerie Fire and Darkness spells.

**Tiefling:** Darkvision. Fire resistance. Free cantrip and spells as you level. Subraces:
- *Asmodeus Tiefling:* Produce Flame, Hellish Rebuke, Darkness.
- *Mephistopheles Tiefling:* Mage Hand, Burning Hands, Flame Blade.
- *Zariel Tiefling:* Thaumaturgy, Searing Smite, Branding Smite.

**Dwarf:** Darkvision. Poison resistance. Proficiency with battleaxes, handaxes, light hammers, warhammers. Subraces:
- *Gold Dwarf:* Extra HP per level — the tankiest race.
- *Shield Dwarf:* Medium armor proficiency — great for casters who want armor.
- *Duergar:* Superior Darkvision. Free Enlarge and Invisibility spells at higher levels.

**Githyanki:** The game's most mechanically rich race. Martial Prodigy gives you proficiency in ALL skills of one ability score plus medium armor and swords. Free spells (Mage Hand, Jump, Misty Step). Githyanki-specific dialogue is abundant and fascinating.

**Gnome:** Advantage on Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma saving throws. Subraces:
- *Rock Gnome:* Expertise in History (doubled proficiency bonus).
- *Forest Gnome:* Free Speak with Animals spell.
- *Deep Gnome:* Superior Darkvision and advantage on Stealth checks.

**Halfling:** Lucky — reroll any 1 on an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw. Must use the new roll. Subraces:
- *Lightfoot Halfling:* Advantage on Stealth checks.
- *Strongheart Halfling:* Poison resistance and advantage against poison.

**Half-Orc:** Relentless Endurance (drop to 1 HP instead of 0, once per long rest). Savage Attacks (extra weapon die on critical hits). Proficiency with Intimidation. The best race for melee damage dealers.

**Dragonborn:** Breath weapon based on your draconic ancestry (fire, cold, acid, lightning, poison). Damage resistance matching your ancestry. Visually striking but mechanically weaker than other races in the base game.

### Classes

Your class defines your combat role and core abilities. You can multiclass (combine two classes) starting at level 2, but for a first playthrough, stick to a single class — it's powerful and simpler to manage.

**Barbarian:** Rage gives you resistance to physical damage and bonus melee damage. Simple, tanky, devastating. Beginner-friendly.
- *Berserker:* Bonus action attacks while raging. Frenzied Throw knocks enemies prone.
- *Wildheart:* Animal-themed rage bonuses (Bear = resist all damage except psychic, Eagle = bonus action dash, Wolf = allies get advantage near you).
- *Wild Magic:* Random magical effects when raging. Chaotic and fun.

**Bard:** Full spellcaster with excellent social skills. Bardic Inspiration buffs allies' rolls. Jack of All Trades adds half your proficiency bonus to all ability checks you're not proficient in.
- *Lore:* Extra magical secrets and Cutting Words (use Bardic Inspiration to debuff enemies). The ultimate skill monkey and support caster.
- *Valor:* Medium armor and shield proficiency. Combat Inspiration adds damage to ally attacks.
- *Swords:* Blade Flourishes that add effects to your weapon attacks (mobile, defensive, slashing). A spellcasting duelist.

**Cleric:** Full divine spellcaster with armor proficiency. Your subclass determines your Domain spells and Channel Divinity abilities. Clerics are versatile — they can heal, blast, control, or tank depending on domain.
- *Life:* The best healer. Heavy armor. All healing spells are boosted.
- *Light:* Fire and radiance blasting. Warding Flare imposes disadvantage on enemy attacks against you.
- *Tempest:* Lightning and thunder magic. Channel Divinity maximizes lightning/thunder damage (guaranteed max damage roll). Heavy armor.
- *War:* Bonus action attacks. Heavy armor and martial weapons. A frontline battle cleric.

**Druid:** Full nature spellcaster with Wild Shape (transform into animals). Versatile but complex.
- *Circle of the Land:* Extra spells and spell slot recovery. The caster-focused druid.
- *Circle of the Moon:* Bonus action Wild Shape and stronger beast forms. Combat shapeshifting.
- *Circle of Spores:* Necrotic damage aura and fungal zombie summons. A melee/caster hybrid.

**Fighter:** The simplest martial class. Action Surge gives you an extra action once per short rest — incredibly powerful. Extra attacks at levels 5 and 11. Beginner-friendly.
- *Battle Master:* Combat maneuvers (disarm, trip, riposte, precision attack) powered by Superiority Dice. Tactical, versatile, and powerful.
- *Champion:* Improved critical hit range (crit on 19-20, then 18-20). Simple and effective.
- *Eldritch Knight:* Fighter with limited Wizard spellcasting. Weapon Bond makes your weapon return when thrown.

**Monk:** Unarmed martial artist who uses Ki points for bonus attacks and abilities. High mobility. Unarmored Defense scales off Dexterity and Wisdom.
- *Way of the Open Hand:* Enhanced unarmed strikes that can stagger, push, or topple enemies.
- *Way of Shadow:* Teleport between shadows. Stealth and mobility focused.
- *Way of the Four Elements:* Spend Ki to cast elemental spells. Thematic but Ki-hungry.

**Paladin:** Holy warrior combining martial prowess with divine spellcasting. Divine Smite spends spell slots for massive radiant damage on hit. Aura of Protection at level 6 adds your Charisma modifier to all saving throws for you and nearby allies — one of the best defensive features in the game. Beginner-friendly.
- *Oath of Devotion:* Classic holy knight. Sacred Weapon adds Charisma to attack rolls.
- *Oath of the Ancients:* Nature-themed paladin. Aura of Warding gives spell damage resistance to nearby allies.
- *Oath of Vengeance:* Offensive paladin. Vow of Enmity gives advantage against one enemy for 10 turns.
- *Oathbreaker:* Hidden subclass gained by breaking your oath. Control undead, aura of hate (adds Charisma to melee damage for you and nearby fiends/undead).

**Ranger:** Martial class with nature magic. Favored Enemy and Natural Explorer choices at level 1 define your specialty.
- *Hunter:* Extra damage options against wounded or grouped enemies. Straightforward and effective.
- *Beast Master:* Summon animal companions that scale with your level. Bears, wolves, ravens, spiders, and more.
- *Gloom Stalker:* Bonus to initiative. Extra attack on round one. Invisible to enemies that rely on darkvision. The stealth archer par excellence.

**Rogue:** Skill expert with Sneak Attack (extra d6s of damage when attacking with advantage or when an ally is adjacent). Cunning Action lets you Dash, Disengage, or Hide as a bonus action.
- *Thief:* Extra bonus action. Fast Hands. The ultimate utility rogue and dual-wielder.
- *Assassin:* Automatic critical hits against surprised enemies. Advantage against enemies that haven't taken a turn yet. Devastating first-round burst.
- *Arcane Trickster:* Rogue with Wizard spellcasting. Invisible Mage Hand that can pickpocket and interact with objects from range.

**Sorcerer:** Full spellcaster who manipulates magic with Metamagic — spend Sorcery Points to quicken spells (bonus action cast), twin spells (double target), heighten spells (impose disadvantage on saves), and more. Fewer spells known than Wizards, but more potent casting.
- *Draconic Bloodline:* Dragon ancestry gives you natural armor, extra HP, and bonus damage of your chosen element.
- *Wild Magic:* Random magical surges when casting. High variance — can win or lose fights on a surge.
- *Storm Sorcery:* Fly as a bonus action after casting a spell. Lightning and thunder themed.

**Warlock:** Spellcaster with a limited number of spell slots that refresh on short rests (always cast at max level). Eldritch Blast cantrip scales with your level and is the best damage cantrip in the game. Pact Boon at level 3 defines your secondary specialty.
- *The Fiend:* Dark One's Blessing gives you temporary HP on kills. Fire spells. Straightforward blaster.
- *The Great Old One:* Mortal Reminder frightens enemies on critical hits. Psychic and control themed.
- *The Archfey:* Fey Presence charms or frightens enemies in an area. Misty Escape lets you teleport and go invisible when hit.

**Wizard:** Full spellcaster with the largest spell list in the game. Learn spells from scrolls. Prepare spells from your known list each day. Arcane Recovery restores spell slots on short rest.
- *Evocation:* Sculpt Spells lets you exclude allies from your AoE (fireball your friends safely!). Bonus damage on evocation spells.
- *Abjuration:* Arcane Ward absorbs damage. Counterspell and Dispel Magic specialist.
- *Divination:* Portent dice — replace any attack roll or saving throw with pre-rolled d20s. Two guaranteed rolls per long rest. Incredibly powerful.
- *Necromancy:* Better undead summons. Heal when you kill with spells.

### Backgrounds

Your background gives you two skill proficiencies and determines how you earn Inspiration (reroll tokens). Pick a background that covers skills your class doesn't provide:

- **Acolyte:** Insight, Religion. Inspiration from acts of faith.
- **Charlatan:** Deception, Sleight of Hand. Inspiration from cons and manipulation.
- **Criminal:** Deception, Stealth. Inspiration from theft and crime.
- **Entertainer:** Acrobatics, Performance. Inspiration from entertaining crowds.
- **Folk Hero:** Animal Handling, Survival. Inspiration from helping the common folk.
- **Guild Artisan:** Insight, Persuasion. Inspiration from crafting and commerce.
- **Noble:** History, Persuasion. Inspiration from aristocratic behavior.
- **Outlander:** Athletics, Survival. Inspiration from exploration and nature.
- **Sage:** Arcana, History. Inspiration from discovering knowledge.
- **Soldier:** Athletics, Intimidation. Inspiration from martial prowess.
- **Urchin:** Sleight of Hand, Stealth. Inspiration from street smarts and survival.

### Ability Scores

These six stats define everything your character can do. At character creation, you have 27 points to distribute via point-buy (recommended for beginners) or you can roll dice.

| Score | Modifier | Description |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| 8 | -1 | Below average |
| 10 | 0 | Average |
| 12 | +1 | Above average |
| 14 | +2 | Skilled |
| 16 | +3 | Expert (max at creation) |
| 17 | +3 | Expert (if using +1 from racial) |
| 20 | +5 | Peak (max in-game) |

**Strength (STR):** Melee attack and damage rolls. Jump distance. Shove success. Carrying capacity. Athletics skill. Primary stat for Barbarians, Fighters (STR builds), Paladins.

**Dexterity (DEX):** Armor Class (if wearing light or medium armor). Initiative bonus. Ranged and finesse weapon attack/damage rolls. Acrobatics, Sleight of Hand, Stealth skills. Primary stat for Rangers, Rogues, Monks, and DEX Fighters.

**Constitution (CON):** HP per level. Concentration saving throws for spellcasters. No skills use CON, but every character needs it. Aim for 14 minimum on every character.

**Intelligence (INT):** Wizard spellcasting. Arcana, History, Investigation, Nature, Religion skills. Dump stat for most non-Wizard classes.

**Wisdom (WIS):** Cleric, Druid, and Ranger spellcasting. Perception (the most-rolled skill in the game). Insight, Medicine, Survival skills. Important for resisting mind control spells.

**Charisma (CHA):** Bard, Paladin, Sorcerer, and Warlock spellcasting. Persuasion, Deception, Intimidation, Performance skills. Your "Face" stat — determines most dialogue outcomes. Even if you're not a CHA caster, don't dump it below 10 on your main character.

**Beginner starting array (27 point-buy):** 16 in your class's primary stat, 14 in CON, 14 in DEX (or secondary stat), 10 in WIS, 10 in remaining, 8 in dump stat. For example, a Fighter: STR 16, DEX 14, CON 14, INT 8, WIS 10, CHA 10.

## Recommended Beginner Builds

These three builds are simple, effective, and teach core game mechanics. Each requires no multiclassing and no complex gear dependencies.

### Build 1: The Commander — Battle Master Fighter

**Playstyle:** Frontline melee warrior. Tank hits. Control enemies with maneuvers. Simple to play, devastatingly effective.

- **Race:** Half-Orc (Savage Attacks + Relentless Endurance = better crits and a second life).
- **Class:** Fighter 12.
- **Subclass:** Battle Master (level 3).
- **Ability Scores:** STR 17, DEX 14, CON 16, INT 8, WIS 10, CHA 8.
- **Fighting Style (Level 1):** Great Weapon Fighting (reroll 1s and 2s on two-handed weapon damage dice).
- **Maneuvers (Level 3):** Precision Attack (add d8 to attack roll), Riposte (counterattack on miss), Menacing Attack (add d8 damage + frighten).
- **Maneuvers (Level 7):** Trip Attack (knock prone), Disarming Attack (force weapon drop).
- **Maneuvers (Level 10):** Goading Attack (taunt), Pushing Attack (shove with damage).
- **Feats:**
  - Level 4: Great Weapon Master (+10 damage per hit at -5 to hit. Use Precision Attack to offset the penalty).
  - Level 6: Ability Score Increase (+2 STR to reach 20 with a certain Act 1 item).
  - Level 8: Alert (+5 initiative — always go first).
  - Level 12: Heavy Armor Master or Savage Attacker.
- **Gear Priorities:** Biggest two-handed weapon you can find (greatsword, greataxe, maul). Heavy armor. Anything that increases attack rolls or adds damage dice.
- **Party Role:** Frontline damage dealer and off-tank. Between Action Surge, Extra Attack x3, and Great Weapon Master, you can delete priority targets in one turn.

### Build 2: The Burning Light — Light Domain Cleric

**Playstyle:** Support caster who also deals massive AoE damage. Heal allies, buff the party, and incinerate enemies with holy fire.

- **Race:** Wood Half-Elf (extra movement + Stealth proficiency + shield proficiency).
- **Class:** Cleric 12.
- **Subclass:** Light Domain (level 1 — chosen during character creation for Clerics).
- **Ability Scores:** STR 8, DEX 14, CON 16, INT 10, WIS 17, CHA 8.
- **Cantrips:** Guidance (THE most important cantrip — add d4 to all skill checks out of combat), Sacred Flame (radiant damage, DEX save), Thaumaturgy (intimidation/performance advantage).
- **Domain Spells:** Burning Hands, Faerie Fire, Flaming Sphere, Scorching Ray, Fireball, Daylight, Wall of Fire, Flame Strike — all automatically prepared.
- **Warding Flare (Level 1):** Reaction to impose disadvantage on an enemy attack. Use every turn you're targeted.
- **Radiance of the Dawn (Level 2):** Channel Divinity — AoE radiant damage that scales with level. Destroys undead and clears magical darkness.
- **Feats:**
  - Level 4: Ability Score Increase (+2 WIS to reach 20 with a certain Act 1 item).
  - Level 8: War Caster (advantage on concentration saves — protect your Spirit Guardians/Bless).
  - Level 12: Resilient: Constitution (proficiency in CON saves for concentration).
- **Gear Priorities:** Medium armor + shield. Any item that boosts spell save DC or adds radiant/fire damage. Luminous Armor (Act 1 Underdark) is your best-in-slot — it radiates a Shockwave on dealing radiant damage.
- **Party Role:** Healer, buffer (Bless is the best level 1 spell in the game), AoE blaster. Cast Spirit Guardians at level 5, then walk through enemies like a radiant lawnmower.

### Build 3: The Silver Tongue — College of Swords Bard

**Playstyle:** A hybrid who does everything — cast spells, fight in melee or at range, pick locks, talk through any situation. The ultimate "face" character who can handle any challenge.

- **Race:** Drow (Superior Darkvision, free Faerie Fire and Darkness, proficiency with rapiers and hand crossbows).
- **Class:** Bard 12.
- **Subclass:** College of Swords (level 3).
- **Ability Scores:** STR 8, DEX 17, CON 14, INT 8, WIS 10, CHA 16.
- **Skills:** Persuasion, Deception, Sleight of Hand, Perception, Performance, Insight, Acrobatics (Bards get 3 of these + 2 from background — pick Charlatan for Deception and Sleight of Hand).
- **Spells (Level 1):** Vicious Mockery (cantrip — psychic damage + disadvantage on next attack. Hilarious and effective). Dissonant Whispers (psychic damage + fear). Healing Word (bonus action heal at range). Faerie Fire (from Drow — AoE advantage). Tasha's Hideous Laughter (single target hard CC).
- **Spells (Magical Secrets at level 10):** Pick Counterspell (shut down enemy casters) and Spirit Guardians (AoE radiant damage aura).
- **Fighting Style (Level 3):** Two-Weapon Fighting (add DEX to off-hand damage).
- **Blade Flourishes:** Slashing Flourish (hit two targets with one attack), Defensive Flourish (add AC for a turn), Mobile Flourish (push enemy and teleport after them).
- **Feats:**
  - Level 4: Ability Score Increase (+2 DEX to reach 20 with Hag Hair in Act 1).
  - Level 8: Sharpshooter (if ranged focus) or Ability Score Increase (+2 CHA).
  - Level 12: Alert or War Caster.
- **Gear Priorities:** Rapier + shield or dual hand crossbows. Light armor. Helmet of Arcane Acuity (Act 2, Mason's Guild) — weapon attacks build Arcane Acuity stacks that boost your spell save DC. Band of the Mystic Scoundrel (Act 3) — cast illusion/enchantment spells as a bonus action after a weapon attack.
- **Party Role:** Party face (handles all social encounters). Skill monkey (pick locks, disarm traps, spot secrets). Offensive support (control spells + weapon damage). Can sub in as healer with Healing Word.

## Important Mechanics

Here are the systems you'll interact with constantly. Understanding these will take you from confused to confident.

### Action Economy

Every character gets one **Action**, one **Bonus Action**, and one **Reaction** per turn in combat. Managing these is the core tactical skill of BG3.

| Resource | What It's Used For | Examples |
|----------|-------------------|----------|
| **Action** | Main attack, most spells, Dash, Disengage, Hide, Help, Use Object | Attack with weapon; cast Fireball; shove an enemy; throw a potion |
| **Bonus Action** | Certain spells, off-hand attack, class features, jump, shove (some builds), drink potion | Healing Word; Cunning Action (Rogues); off-hand crossbow shot; Jump |
| **Reaction** | Triggered by conditions, once per round | Opportunity Attack (enemy leaves melee range); Counterspell; Shield spell; Warding Flare |

**Key tip:** You can't use a Bonus Action as an Action or vice versa. If you have nothing to do with your Bonus Action, you can always Jump (gives extra mobility) or drink a potion.

### Short Rest vs. Long Rest

- **Short Rest:** Heals half your HP (rounded up). Restores Warlock spell slots, Fighter's Action Surge and Second Wind, Monk's Ki, and some other class resources. Takes no resources. You get 2 short rests per long rest.
- **Long Rest:** Fully heals the party. Restores ALL spell slots and class resources. Costs 40 Camp Supplies (or 80 on Tactician/Honor Mode). The most important thing: **long rests advance companion storylines.** Camp conversations, romance scenes, and character development often trigger during long rests. Don't hoard them — use them frequently.

**Camp Supplies:** Food items you find everywhere. Right-click any food and select "Send to Camp" to store it. The game auto-selects enough food to meet the supply cost. You'll have more than you need by mid-Act 1.

### Inspiration

Inspiration is a free reroll token. You earn it by performing actions that align with your character's Background goals (e.g., a Sage earns Inspiration for discovering ancient lore). You can hold up to 4 Inspiration points.

When you fail an important ability check (in dialogue or exploration), the game offers you the option to spend an Inspiration point for a reroll. **Use these on critical story checks** — convincing a boss, detecting a trap before it wipes your party, persuading a companion. Don't waste them on minor checks.

You can see your current Inspiration points by the small diamond icons next to the minimap.

### Dialogue Checks

During conversations, you'll frequently see bracketed options like **[Persuasion]**, **[Deception]**, **[Intimidation]**, **[Insight]**, **[Arcana]**, etc. These are ability checks that use your character's skills.

- **[Persuasion / Deception / Intimidation]:** Your Charisma skills. Try to convince, lie to, or threaten NPCs. Charisma classes (Bard, Paladin, Sorcerer, Warlock) excel here.
- **[Insight]:** Wisdom-based. Detect lies, read emotions. Clerics and Druids excel.
- **[Arcana / History / Nature / Religion]:** Intelligence-based. Recall lore. Wizards excel.
- **[Perception / Investigation]:** Spot hidden details. High Wisdom characters notice more.

Before attempting a difficult check: cast **Guidance** (adds d4), use **Bardic Inspiration** (adds d6/d8/d10), or use **Enhance Ability** (advantage on checks of one ability). Stack these buffs for the hardest checks.

**Failing isn't game over.** BG3 is designed around failure. Failed checks often lead to interesting alternative outcomes. Don't save-scum every failed check — the game is richer when you roll with the consequences.

### Skill Checks

Outside of dialogue, you make skill checks for exploration: picking locks (Sleight of Hand), spotting traps (Perception), disarming traps (Sleight of Hand with Thieves' Tools), sneaking past enemies (Stealth), reading ancient texts (Arcana), and more.

**Proficiency bonus** doubles the bonus from your proficiency in a skill. At level 1, proficiency = +2. You are proficient in all skills from your class, background, and race. Your skill modifier = Ability modifier + Proficiency bonus (if proficient).

Example: A Rogue with DEX 16 (+3) and proficiency in Sleight of Hand (+2) has a total Sleight of Hand bonus of +5 at level 1.

### Saving Throws

When a spell or effect targets you, you often make a saving throw to resist it. Each class is proficient in two saving throws:

| Class | Proficient Saves |
|-------|-----------------|
| Barbarian | STR, CON |
| Bard | DEX, CHA |
| Cleric | WIS, CHA |
| Druid | INT, WIS |
| Fighter | STR, CON |
| Monk | STR, DEX |
| Paladin | WIS, CHA |
| Ranger | STR, DEX |
| Rogue | DEX, INT |
| Sorcerer | CON, CHA |
| Warlock | WIS, CHA |
| Wizard | INT, WIS |

The enemy's Spell Save DC is the number you need to meet or beat on your d20 + save modifier roll.

### Concentration

Many powerful spells require concentration — you can only concentrate on ONE spell at a time. When you take damage while concentrating, you make a Constitution saving throw (DC = 10 or half the damage taken, whichever is higher). On a failure, the spell ends.

**Key concentration spells worth protecting:**
- Bless (level 1) — your entire party's accuracy boost
- Spirit Guardians (level 3) — AoE radiant damage aura
- Haste (level 3) — double a character's actions
- Hold Person/Monster (level 2/5) — paralyze an enemy
- Hunger of Hadar (level 3) — zone of blindness and cold/acid damage
- Wall of Fire (level 4) — line of fire damage

If you're concentrating on something important: stay out of melee, use the Dodge action, drink an Elixir of Peerless Focus (advantage on CON saves), or take the War Caster feat.

### Advantage and Disadvantage

**Advantage:** Roll two d20s and take the higher result. Statistically worth about +3.3 to +5. Gained from: attacking from stealth, attacking prone/blinded/paralyzed enemies, high ground (ranged attacks), Faerie Fire, Guiding Bolt, Reckless Attack (Barbarian), etc.

**Disadvantage:** Roll two d20s and take the lower result. Gained from: attacking at long range, attacking prone enemies (ranged), attacking while blinded or in darkness, poisoned, frightened, etc.

Advantage and disadvantage cancel each other out (no matter how many sources of each exist). You either have advantage, disadvantage, or neither — they don't stack.

## Combat Fundamentals

### Initiative

At the start of combat, everyone rolls a **d4** (not a d20 as in tabletop 5e) and adds their Dexterity modifier. Higher initiative goes first. Tied initiative goes to the higher Dexterity score.

**This means:** The Alert feat (+5 initiative) is incredibly powerful in BG3. A character with Alert and DEX 14 will roll d4 + 7 — almost always going first. Going first means killing or controlling enemies before they act.

Initiative order is shown as portraits at the top of the screen. Characters sharing the same initiative can act in any order — you can freely switch between them.

### Positioning

Your position on the battlefield matters enormously:

- **High Ground:** Ranged attacks from high ground get +2 to attack rolls (advantage equivalent). Ranged attacks against targets on high ground get no penalty. Always take the high ground if you can.
- **Backstab / Flanking:** Attacking an enemy from behind doesn't give advantage in BG3 (unlike tabletop 5e). However, **Sneak Attack** (Rogue) only triggers if you have advantage OR an ally is adjacent to the target. Position your melee allies near your Rogue's target.
- **Threatened:** If you're in melee range of an enemy, your ranged attacks have **disadvantage**. Casters: use Shocking Grasp (melee cantrip) or disengage before casting ranged spells.
- **Opportunity Attacks:** If you leave an enemy's melee range without using Disengage, they get a free attack. Rogues and Monks can Disengage as a bonus action — use it.

### Surfaces

BG3's environmental interactions are a core tactical layer. The ground itself can become a hazard:

| Surface | Created By | Effect |
|---------|-----------|--------|
| **Fire** | Fire spells, Fire Bolt, Alchemist's Fire, exploding barrels | d4 fire damage per turn. Ignites oil and grease. |
| **Ice** | Ice spells, water + cold damage | DEX save or fall prone. Difficult terrain. Melts into water from fire. |
| **Acid** | Acid Splash, Chromatic Orb: Acid, Acid Vial | -2 AC on affected creatures. Minor acid damage. |
| **Lightning / Electrified Water** | Lightning spells hitting water or steam | d4 lightning damage per turn. Stuns on failed save. |
| **Grease** | Grease spell, Grease Bottle | DEX save or fall prone. Ignites from any fire source. |
| **Web** | Web spell, Spider webs | Difficult terrain. DEX save or Enwebbed (can't move). Burns away from fire. |
| **Darkness / Fog** | Darkness spell, Fog Cloud, Hunger of Hadar | Heavily obscured — blinded while inside, attacks in/out have disadvantage. |

**Combo examples:**
- Cast Create Water (level 1) on a group, then use a lightning spell — electrified water hits everyone.
- Throw a Grease Bottle, then Fire Bolt it — instant AoE fire surface.
- Cast Hunger of Hadar (blinds + cold/acid damage in a zone), then have your martial with Sentinel stand at the edge — enemies leaving the zone trigger opportunity attacks.

### Shove, Jump, and Throw

These are bonus actions available to everyone. Martials (especially Barbarians and STR Fighters) use them constantly.

- **Shove:** Push an enemy 1-5m (based on your Athletics vs. their Athletics/Acrobatics). Pushing enemies off cliffs, into lava, or into your Hunger of Hadar zone is one of the most fun and effective tactics in BG3. STR-based characters are better at shoving.
- **Jump:** Leap to a location using your movement. Extra jump distance for STR characters. Use to cross gaps, reach high ground, or escape melee without provoking opportunity attacks (jumping doesn't trigger them).
- **Throw:** Throw an item, weapon, or even a small enemy (STR check vs their weight). Returning weapons (like the Eldritch Knight's Weapon Bond or specific magic items) return to your hand after throwing. Throw healing potions at downed allies to revive them from range.

### Helping Downed Allies

When a character drops to 0 HP, they're "downed" — they can't act and must make Death Saving Throws each turn (DC 10, three successes to stabilize, three failures to die).

To help them: use the **Help** action (1 Action) while adjacent to bring them back to 1 HP. Alternatively, throw a healing potion at them, or cast Healing Word (bonus action, ranged). Always prioritize reviving downed allies — a character at 1 HP is worth more than a dead character.

## Early-Game Tips

### Party Composition

A balanced party has four roles covered:

1. **Face:** High Charisma for dialogue checks. Usually your main character. Persuasion, Deception, Intimidation.
2. **Lockpicker / Trap Disarmer:** High Dexterity, proficiency in Sleight of Hand. Usually a Rogue or DEX-based character.
3. **Support / Healer:** Can heal, buff, and control enemies. Cleric, Bard, or Druid.
4. **Damage Dealer(s):** Your remaining slots. Mix physical and magical damage.

**Example starting party with the Commander build (Fighter):**
- Tav: Battle Master Fighter (face via Soldier background Intimidation)
- Shadowheart: Trickery Cleric (healer/buffer — respecc her to Light or Life domain at Withers for better results)
- Astarion: Rogue (lockpicker, ranged damage with Sneak Attack)
- Gale: Wizard (AoE blasting, control spells, Arcana checks)

### Key Act 1 Companions — Where to Find Them

- **Shadowheart:** On the nautiloid. If you miss her, she's unconscious on the beach after the crash. **Cannot miss** — she finds you.
- **Astarion:** West of the crashed nautiloid, near the cliff path. He'll ask for help with an "intellect devourer." Talk to him.
- **Gale:** At the Roadside Cliffs waypoint, in the glowing purple sigil on the rock wall. Interact with the malfunctioning portal.
- **Lae'zel:** North of the Roadside Cliffs, trapped in a wooden cage by two tieflings. Free her (or don't). She's near the cage, or you'll find her later near the Mountain Pass.
- **Wyll:** Inside the Druid Grove, training tiefling children. Talk to him after the goblin attack at the gate.
- **Karlach:** East of the Risen Road, near the tollhouse along the river. Follow the trail of blood and burned ground. Bring Wyll for unique dialogue.

### Before Leaving the Nautiloid

- Explore every room. Loot all corpses and containers.
- Free Shadowheart from the pod using the console (Arcana check or find the key).
- In the final room, Commander Zhalk (the cambion fighting the mind flayer) drops the **Everburn Blade** — a greatsword that deals extra fire damage. To get it: have Shadowheart cast Command: Drop on Zhalk. This is a difficult check but extremely rewarding. Save before attempting.
- Grab the three Intellect Devourers (Us) if you find them — they're temporary companions on the ship.

### Camp Supplies

Food is everywhere — barrels, crates, tables, corpses. Right-click food items and "Send to Camp" to store them. You need 40 supplies per long rest. By the end of Act 1 you'll have hundreds. Don't ration your rests — the story progresses when you rest.

### Respeccing

If you don't like your build, Withers at camp will respec you for 100 gold. This resets you to level 1 (you keep your XP and can re-level immediately). Change your class, ability scores, spells, everything. There's zero penalty. Feel free to experiment.

## Common Pitfalls

### Friendly Fire is Real

AoE spells (Fireball, Lightning Bolt, Hunger of Hadar, Cloud of Daggers, etc.) hit allies in the area. Check the targeting indicator — blue circles are allies, red are enemies. The Evocation Wizard's Sculpt Spells feature is the only way to cast Fireball on a melee scrum without roasting your Fighter.

### Failing Dialogue Checks Isn't Game Over

This is the most important advice for new players: the game is designed around failure. Some of the most memorable moments come from failed checks. The story adapts. Don't feel pressure to save-scum every Persuasion check. Let the dice tell a story.

### Respeccing is Cheap

See above — Withers, 100g, full respec. If a build isn't clicking, change it. You're never locked in.

### Use Your Consumables

Scrolls, potions, poisons, arrows, grenades, elixirs — use them. The game throws consumables at you constantly. You'll find more than you can ever use. Hoarding them "for later" means never using them. Throw that grease bottle. Drink that Speed Potion. Fire that Arrow of Many Targets.

### Long Rest Often

New players often try to minimize rests because other games punish resting. BG3 does the opposite — the story advances during camp scenes. If you're going 5+ fights without resting, you're probably missing companion dialogue, romance progression, and story beats. As a rule of thumb: long rest every 2-3 fights early on, then every 1-2 fights by Act 2.

### Read Your Abilities

The tooltips in BG3 are detailed and accurate. Hover over everything. Press T while hovering to pin a tooltip. Read what your spells actually do — conditions, damage types, saving throws. The game rewards system knowledge.
